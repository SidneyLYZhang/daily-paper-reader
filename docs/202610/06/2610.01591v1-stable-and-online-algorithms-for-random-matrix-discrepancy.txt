Eren C. Kızıldağ ∗ Shuangping Li †

Abstract 

We study the average-case matrix discrepancy problem: given independent normalized 

d × d Gaussian orthogonal ensemble matrices A1, . . . , A N and a fixed margin κ > 0, find signs 

σ1, . . . , σ N ∈ {− 1, 1} such that the operator norm of PNi=1 σiAi is at most κ√N . Focusing on the proportional regime N/d 2 → τ ∈ (0 , ∞) as d → ∞ followed by the small-margin limit κ ↓ 0,we characterize the density required by stable offline algorithms and by online algorithms. In the offline setting, we construct a polynomial-time recenter-and-round algorithm that is noise-stable and succeeds whenever τ = Ω( 1

At the core of our algorithms lies rotational symmetry, which enables us to transfer Frobenius norm control into operator norm guarantees. Prior work Choosing the signs independently and uniformly at random yields an O(√N log m)

discrepancy, which becomes O(√N log N ) for m = Θ( N ). Removing the √log N factor turned out to be a major challenge. Building on the partial coloring technique of Beck [Bec81], Spencer’s celebrated 

‘six standard deviations suffice’ theorem [Spe85] improved this guarantee to O(pN log(2 m/N )) 

(m ≥ N ). For the square case, this is optimal up to constants as there are N vectors in [−1, 1] N

with discrepancy Ω( √N ).1

Spencer’s original proof was non-constructive. Obtaining efficient (i.e., polynomial-time) algo-rithms for finding signings achieving small discrepancy required decades of further progress. In a 2010 breakthrough, Bansal [Ban10] gave the first polynomial-time construction attaining Spencer’s bound up to constants for m = Θ( N ), while Lovett and Meka [LM12] subsequently developed a constructive partial coloring framework attaining the full Spencer bound, and Rothvoß [Rot14] extended this framework to general symmetric convex sets. The connection to convex geometry is also central to Banaszczyk’s vector-balancing theorem [Ban98] and its algorithmic realization through the Gram–Schmidt walk [BDGL18]. Another longstanding problem in discrepancy theory was the Komlós conjecture (recorded by Spencer [Spe87]), which asserts that there exists a universal C > 0 such that every collection 

v1, . . . , v N ∈ Rm with ∥vi∥2 ≤ 1 admits signs σi ∈ {− 1, 1} satisfying ∥ PNi=1 σivi∥∞ ≤ C. Following recent progress by Bansal and Jiang [BJ26a, BJ26b], Guo, Fang, and Lu [GFL26] resolved the conjecture; see also a simplified proof by Karingula and Lovett [KL26] and an extension to complex-valued signs ( σi ∈ C, |σi| = 1 ) by Guillen and Kobzar [GK26].

For a symmetric matrix M , ∥M ∥op =sup ∥u∥2=1 |uT M u |, so one must simultaneously control an infinite family of quadratic forms in every direction. Unlike diagonal matrices, general symmetric input matrices need not share an eigenbasis. Thus, the operator-norm constraint cannot be reduced to balancing d fixed coordinate directions in general. Extending available methods (e.g., partial coloring) to this setting has therefore motivated new geometric estimates for the associated discrepancy bodies; see [RR20, DJR22]. Despite substantial progress for structured inputs [LRR17, BKMZ24, BB26, AS26a], the general Matrix Spencer conjecture remained a major open problem for over a decade. In the square case (d = N ), Hopkins, Raghavendra and Shetty [HRS22] resolved the conjecture under an additional assumption ∥Ai∥F ≤ N 1/4 using ideas from quantum communication complexity. Dadush, Jiang and Reis [DJR22] developed a mirror descent framework to obtain partial colorings, resolving the conjecture for block-diagonal matrices with block side O(N/d ), while Bansal, Jiang, and Meka [BJM23] resolved the conjecture when each matrix has rank at most O(N/ log 3 N ) using refined matrix concentration inequalities [BBvH23]. A series of breakthroughs beginning in August 2026 completely resolved the Matrix Spencer conjecture and brought substantial algorithmic advances. Akbas and Sra [AS26c] gave a constructive proof in the square case; their exposition was subsequently simplified by Bansal and Kook [BK26]. Soon after, Song and Zhang [SZ26] obtained a randomized algorithm with almost-linear runtime in the dense input size measured in the real-arithmetic model. Kathuria [Kat26a] established the conjectured rectangular dependence with a randomized polynomial-time algorithm and, in a companion work [Kat26b], gave a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for Weaver’s discrepancy theorem, both in the real-arithmetic model; see also the deterministic algorithm of Ezeunala and Jiang [EJ26]. Originally introduced by Weaver [Wea04], the latter model was instrumental in the resolution of Kadison–Singer problem from functional analysis by Marcus, Spielman, and Srivas-tava [MSS15]; see also rank-one discrepancy bounds [KLS20] and worst-case hardness results [SZ22]. Even more recently, Akbas and Sra [AS26b] developed small-ball inequalities for Boolean matrix series, recovering various high-profile problems including Kadison–Singer, Matrix Spencer, and Komlós conjectures as corollaries. 

Average-case setting Another line of research studies discrepancy minimization for random inputs rather than worst-case instances. Specifying an input distribution allows one to investigate typical optimum, phase transitions, and the limits of efficient algorithms in finer detail. For independent standard normal vectors v1, . . . , v N ∼ N (0 , I d), nonconstructive arguments yield that disc( v1, . . . , v N ) = Θ( √N 2−N/d ) with high probability (whp) whenever d = o(N ) [TMR20, Cos09, KKLO86]. By contrast, the best known polynomial-time algorithm achieves only 2−Ω(log 2 N/d ),provided d grows sufficiently slow [TMR20, Yak96, KK83]. Substantial separation between these existential and algorithmic guarantees illustrates a conjectured statistical–computational gap : finding near-optimal signings efficiently may be hard. For rigorous evidences supporting this conjec-ture, see [GK23, VV25]. Discrete input distributions have also received considerable attention; e.g., [ANW22] obtained constant-discrepancy guarantees for Bernoulli/Poisson random vectors. A closely related model is the symmetric binary perceptron (SBP) [APZ19] which asks for a signing with discrepancy at most κ√N (for fixed κ > 0) in the proportional regime d = Θ( N ).For this model, prior work characterized the satisfiability threshold precisely and yielded sharp algorithmic lower bounds for restricted classes of algorithms. Starting from independent random signs, Algorithm 1 iteratively solves a strongly convex quadratic program over [−1, 1] N that balances the Euclidean residual against the expected number of sign flips, then applies independent, mean-preserving rounding to obtain binary signs. The quadratic regularization term makes the objective function strongly convex, ensuring a unique minimizer and enabling the noise-stability analysis. Our analysis exploits a key geometric property of independent Gaussian columns: with high probability, every direction admits sufficiently many columns with large positive projections. Proved using learning-theoretic tools (VC inequality and Sauer’s lemma), this property enables fractional cancellations with a small number of flips, and obtaining a contraction estimate for the residual. The margin limit was also important in pinning down the algorithmic landscape for asymmetric binary perceptron [LSZ25] (see also related works [JL26, MZZ24]). The structure of the solution space in binary perceptron models and its relationship to algorithmic tractability have also been extensively studied [BIL +15, PX21, ALS22b, ALS22a, BEKZ24, GHLS26]. To compare these results with ours, recall that the space of d × d symmetric matrices has dimension D = d(d + 1) /2. The corresponding dimension-to-sign ratio is therefore D/N → (2 τ )−1.Under this convention, our stable offline and online transition scales (Theorems 1.1 and 1.2) respectively become Θ( κ2 log(1 /κ )) and Θ( κ2), thereby matching the corresponding algorithmic thresholds of the SBP. Furthermore, Maillard’s satisfiability scale [Mai25] becomes log 2 / log(1 /κ ),agreeing with the SBP even at the level of the leading constant. Despite replacing coordinate-wise balance by a noncommutative spectral constraint, random matrix discrepancy therefore exhibits the same order and hierarchy of the small margin algorithmic scales as the SBP. More recently, Huang, Sellke and Sun [HSS26] studied the empirical projection distributions attainable by Lipschitz algorithms through stochastic control, and obtained exact variational characterizations of algorithmic thresholds for various perceptron models with bounded continuous activations; see also the earlier stochastic-control characterization by Montanari and Zhou [MZ24] and Sun’s ICM survey [Sun26]. For the SBP, their arguments settle the threshold for stable Lipschitz algorithms at every fixed κ > 0, suggesting a natural direction for matrix discrepancy: characterizing its algorithmic thresholds at fixed positive κ > 0 beyond the small margin asymptotics. 

Related work The study of random matrix discrepancy was initiated by Kunisky and Zhang [KZ23], who obtained upper and lower bounds on the discrepancy (1), and analyzed the matrix hyperbolic cosine (MHC) algorithm of Zouzias [Zou12]. Their bounds identify the order of the typical discrepancy for normalized GOE inputs in the proportional regime N = Θ( d2). Kunisky, Oertel, Wengiel, and Zhang [KOWZ25] subsequently established sharp discrepancy bounds when 

d2 = o(N/ log N ) and improved the analysis of the MHC algorithm. Maillard [Mai25] studied the satisfiability variant analogous to the SBP, obtaining lower and upper bounds on the satisfiability threshold in the proportional regime that agree to leading order as κ ↓ 0. Random rank-one inputs were studied in [CCZ25]. On the algorithmic side, the improved MHC analysis yields a terminal discrepancy bound of order O(d log d) for normalized GOE inputs, which is O(√N log N ) when N = Θ( d2). This estimate alone does not certify a fixed-margin guarantee κ√N at any fixed proportional density. Very recently, Chen and El Alaoui [CE26] developed an approximate message passing (AMP) algorithm for random 5matrix discrepancy, together with a novel free-probabilistic state evolution describing its limiting iterates through a correlated Gaussian semicircular process. For N/d 2 → τ , their algorithm achieves a discrepancy of gAMP (τ )√N whp for an explicit gAMP (τ ) ∈ (0 , 2) and small τ , 0 < τ < τ ∗ ≈ 2.87 .In particular, their algorithm yields signs satisfying (2) for margins κ ∈ (gAMP (τ ), 2) . Our work complements these results by addressing the small-margin limit, where κ ↓ 0 and the density τ

grows: we pinpoint the density required by stable offline and online algorithms up to constants, through polynomial-time algorithms and matching lower bounds. 

Online discrepancy Online discrepancy, where the arriving vectors must be assigned signs irrevocably, has a long history dating back to Spencer’s balancing games [Spe77]. For vectors with Euclidean norm at most one, an adaptive adversary can force Ω( √N ) discrepancy even in dimension two. Against an oblivious adversary, which fixes the input sequence in advance, substantially stronger guarantees are possible. Alweiss, Liu, and Sawhney [ALS21] developed a self-balancing walk that maintains O(log( dN )) discrepancy for all signed prefix sums whp, provided ∥vi∥2 ≤ 1.Kulkarni, Reis, and Rothvoß [KRR24] established the existence of an online algorithm attaining the optimal O(√log N ) guarantee; see also an improved running time guarantee by Aden-Ali [AA26]. For the random arrivals, Bansal and Spencer [BS20] gave an online algorithm achieving O(√d)

discrepancy whp for independent uniform vectors in {− 1, 1}d, with a bound independent of the time horizon; see [Wen26] for an extension to sub-Gaussian inputs. For Gaussian arrivals with N ≍ d,[FJLNW26] characterized the optimal asymptotic constant through a mean-field stochastic control problem. More general stochastic arrivals were studied in [BJSS20, BJM +21]; the latter obtained 

O(log 4(dN )) discrepancy for independent samples from an arbitrary distribution supported on the Euclidean unit ball. For a more recent work on online vector balancing with iid binary sparse vectors, see [AT26]. Contrasting online vector balancing, the history of online matrix discrepancy is quite recent. Zouzias [Zou12] introduced the matrix hyperbolic cosine algorithm, whose average-case performance was subsequently analyzed in [KZ23, KOWZ25]. To the best of our knowledge, our results yield the first sharp characterization of an online algorithmic thresholds for the matrix discrepancy problem under random arrivals. 

Overlap geometry and algorithmic hardness Our algorithmic lower bounds are based on the overlap gap property (OGP) framework, pioneered by Gamarnik and Sudan [GS14, GS17a]; see [Gam21, GMZ22, Gam25] for surveys. Pairwise OGP forbids intermediate overlaps between pairs of near-optimal solutions, while ensemble or coupled versions impose overlap restrictions on solutions to correlated instances [CGPR19, GJW24]. Multi-OGP extends this viewpoint to forbidden overlap patterns among larger collections of solutions [RV17, GS17b, Wei21, BH22, GK23], and branching OGP excludes hierarchical overlap patterns [HS25, HS23]. Coupling and interpolation arguments convert these geometric obstructions into lower bounds for local, low-degree, AMP, and Lipschitz algorithms by exploiting suitable notions of input stability [GS17a, GJW24, GJ21, HS25]. More recently, related geometric obstructions have also been developed for online algorithms [GKW25, DKM25, DDK +26, DGH25, BGG26, GRS26]. Our lower bound uses an ensemble multi-OGP for feasible signings, following the perceptron arguments [GKPX22, GKPX23, LSZ25].

Proof ideas Our proof for stable algorithms uses the overlap gap property, previously employed in the context of binary perceptron [GKPX22, GKPX23, LSZ25]. For stable algorithms, we rule out m feasible signings on correlated instances with pairwise overlaps in a narrow interval for an appropriate m ∈ N—dubbed forbidden structure ; we control small ball probabilities of form 

∥Ai∥op ≤ κ√N , ∀i via passing to the Frobenius norm and Gaussian density bounds. We then interpolate from a common Gaussian instance to many independent instances, use stability of the algorithm and apply a multi-color Ramsey-theoretic argument to extract an m-tuple contradicting the forbidden structure . For online algorithms, we condition on the random seed and the first N − q

input matrices, so that the signed prefix is fixed. Each fixed signing of the remaining q matrices gives a Gaussian vector independent of the conditioned data. A uniform shifted small-ball estimate and a union bound over the 2q suffix signings yield an exponentially small success probability. Taking q of order κ2N proves the density lower bound of order κ−2.
