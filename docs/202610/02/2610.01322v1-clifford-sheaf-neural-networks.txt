# Clifford Sheaf Neural Networks 

Kotaro Kamiya ∗ Joel Nicholls †

SyntheticGestalt KK 

Abstract 

We introduce the Clifford Sheaf Neural Network (CSNN), an equivariant sheaf neural network for geometric graphs that places a Clifford algebra on each stalk of a cellular sheaf and transports multivector features along edges. The canonical choice of restriction map for sheaves with algebra-valued stalks is algebra homomorphism. Adding the constraint of equivariance, the naive choice becomes versor conjugation. However, versor conjugation is expressively weak, so we drop algebra homomorphism and arrive at the K-term sandwich. The resulting sheaf Laplacian is positive semidefinite by construction, needs no versor constraint, and still mixes grades. Our main contribution characterizes the resulting family of restriction maps along three axes: which grades a map couples, how much of the endomorphism space it reaches, and how well it is conditioned. The K-term sandwich spans half of the endomorphism space, and in Cl(3 , 0, 0) it corresponds to the maps that commute with the central pseudoscalar. The number of terms controls expressivity. CSNN is the reversion member, a first-order model by construction and the grade-mixing corner of this family, developed as a sheaf construction for graph-level equivariant regression. 

Keywords: Clifford algebra, cellular sheaves, sheaf Laplacian, equivariance, geometric deep learning, multivector representations 

# 1 Introduction 

Many learning problems live on geometric graphs, where a spatial position is assigned to each node. Such problems reward architectures that respect the symmetries of space. Two lines of work each supply a powerful inductive bias. Clifford (geometric) algebra represents scalars, vectors, and higher-grade quantities as a single multivector and equips them with an equivariant geometric product, so a network manipulates geometric features of every grade under one operation (Ruhe et al., 2023a; Brehmer et al., 2023). Cellular sheaves ask neighbours to agree only after a learned transport, not directly. The sheaf Laplacian, which generalizes the graph Laplacian, encodes this and lets message passing move beyond homophily (Hansen and Gebhart, 2020; Bodnar et al., 2022). The two can be combined by placing a Clifford algebra on each stalk and transporting multivectors along edges. But the obvious construction runs into two difficulties. Suppose the restriction maps are Clifford-algebra automorphisms, i.e. conjugation by a group element. First, the sheaf Laplacian behaves well only when the restriction maps are co-isometries, and that holds only for versors , an awkward constraint to maintain during learning. Second, conjugation preserves grades: transport cannot move information between them, and wastes much of what the multivector offers.

CSNN uses the reversion member, which mixes scalar and vector grades but stays confined to them and has single-ratio conditioning. It is first-order by construction and, to our knowledge, the first sheaf construction aimed at graph-level equivariant regression. The result is both a principled construction and a map of its design space.

In Cl(3 , 0, 0), the grades are scalar, vector, bivector, and pseudoscalar, so a node’s scalar feature and its 3D position live in one element, coupled equivariantly by the geometric product. 2.2 Cellular Sheaves and the Sheaf Laplacian 

We view an undirected graph as a cell poset with vertices below the edges they bound (Figure 1). A cellular sheaf assigns to each cell a vector space, its stalk , and to each incident vertex–edge pair a 21-cell 0-cell 0-cell Figure 1: The cell poset for a single edge, viewed as the base space. The admissible family of restriction maps is a modelling choice. Taking the target to be the category of Clifford algebras would force the maps to be algebra automorphisms, which is too restrictive, so we take the target to be FinVect , the category of finite-dimensional vector spaces. Restriction maps are then arbitrary linear maps between stalks, and the network exploits the multivector structure internally rather than as part of the sheaf formalism. The sheaf Laplacian is L = δ∗δ, where the coboundary δ maps node features to edge features, (δv )e = fj→e(vj ) − fi→e(vi), (4) with fk→e the restriction map from node k into edge e. We take its adjoint δ∗ with respect to the Clifford scalar product on stalks, ⟨δv, ω ⟩E = ⟨v, δ ∗ω⟩V , where ⟨· , ·⟩ V and ⟨· , ·⟩ E aggregate the per-stalk product over nodes and over oriented edges. As a Gram operator the Laplacian is positive semidefinite by construction: 

⟨v, Lv⟩V = ⟨δv, δv ⟩E = ∥δv ∥2 ≥ 0. (5) Two facts give (5). The Gram structure L = δ∗δ yields ⟨v, Lv⟩V = ∥δv ∥2 whenever the restriction maps admit an adjoint under the stalk form, with no orthogonality or versor constraint. In the non-degenerate Cl(3 , 0, 0) every linear map does; in the degenerate Cl(3 , 0, 1), where an adjoint exists only for maps preserving the radical, the normalized reversion sandwiches do (Section 4.3). The non-negativity then holds because the Clifford scalar product is positive semidefinite in both signatures; the orthogonal maps of connection-Laplacian sheaves (Barbero et al., 2022) are one special case. The energy ∥δv ∥2 is the sheaf Dirichlet energy (Bodnar et al., 2022), measuring disagreement after transport , so neighbours need agree only up to a learned transport, which carries sheaf models beyond homophily.

3.2 Three Axes: Grade Coupling, Reachability, and Conditioning 

Every member of the sandwich family is equivariant, whatever its form (Section 3.1). Which linear maps it can realize, once we lift the versor constraint, is a separate question, answered by three axes: which grades a map couples, how much of the endomorphism space it reaches, and how well it is conditioned. CSNN’s reversion map is one corner of this family, with conditioning set by a single ratio. 

Grade coupling. The first axis asks which grades a map can mix (Figure 2), and three involutions answer it. Besides reversion ˜x = β(x) and the grade involution α, Cl(3 , 0, 0) carries the Clifford conjugation γ = α ◦ β. Each splits the algebra into ±1 eigenspaces, grouping the grades into pairs: 

β into {0, 1} and {2, 3}, γ into {0, 3} and {1, 2}, and α into {0, 2} and {1, 3}. A map preserving an involution’s eigenspaces cannot couple grades across the corresponding boundary. 

Proposition 2 (Grade confinement) . The normalized reversion sandwich sc preserves the β-eigenspaces for every c, as does any composition of reversion sandwiches. In Cl(3 , 0, 0) these eigenspaces are {0, 1} and {2, 3}, so a network seeded in grade 0 stays confined to grades {0, 1} at every depth. 

4The reversion sandwich thus couples only {0 ↔ 1} and {2 ↔ 3}: it mixes grades, unlike grade-preserving conjugation, but not fully. A single general sandwich a x b with independent a, b preserves no involution and couples all six grade pairs. # 5 First-Order Representational Placement 

Where does CSNN sit among equivariant models? Grade confinement fixes the answer. Under the rotation action the four grades of Cl(3 , 0, 0) carry familiar irreducibles: grades 0 and 3 are spin-0 (invariant), grades 1 and 2 spin-1. CSNN thus operates entirely with L=0 and L=1 representations and carries no rank-two tensors, placing it with scalar–vector models such as PaiNN (Schütt et al., 2021) rather than higher-order tensor models such as Equiformer (Liao and Smidt, 2023). By Proposition 2 this is structural, not incidental: the reversion CSNN is a first-order model by construction. This grade analysis is specific to Cl(3 , 0, 0); in Cl(3 , 0, 1) reversion preserves the 

β-eigenspaces {0, 1, 4} and {2, 3}, with positions (grade 3) in the latter. 

# 6 Related Work 

Clifford and geometric-algebra networks. Many architectures compute with multivector features and the geometric product: Clifford group-equivariant networks (Ruhe et al., 2023a), geometric Clifford algebra networks (Ruhe et al., 2023b), Clifford neural layers for PDEs (Brandstetter et al., 2023), the geometric algebra transformer (Brehmer et al., 2023), and multivector neurons (Liu et al., 2024b). These fix the message-passing form or constrain transformations to the Clifford group; CSNN instead makes edge-wise transport a learned, versor-free restriction map inside a sheaf Laplacian. 

Sheaf neural networks. Sheaf neural networks (Hansen and Gebhart, 2020) and neural sheaf diffusion (Bodnar et al., 2022) attach real vector-space stalks to a graph and learn restriction maps; connection Laplacians (Barbero et al., 2022) force these orthogonal, all for node-level tasks. CSNN gives the stalks Clifford structure and normalized reversion sandwiches that lift orthogonality while keeping an exact positive-semidefinite Laplacian, and targets graph-level regression, where the sheaf-Laplacian kernel becomes a genuine constraint (Section 7). 

Equivariant models for molecules. Scalar–vector ( L=1) models such as EGNN (Satorras et al., 2021) and PaiNN (Schütt et al., 2021), and first-order attention models such as the equivariant transformer (Thölke and De Fabritiis, 2022), share CSNN’s representational class; the higher-order 8tensor models SEGNN (Brandstetter et al., 2022), tensor field networks (Thomas et al., 2018), MACE (Batatia et al., 2022), and Equiformer (Liao and Smidt, 2023) use L≥2 features. 

Gauge equivariance and topological message passing. Reading restriction maps as parallel transport connects CSNN to gauge-equivariant networks (Cohen et al., 2019; de Haan et al., 2021), and its cell-poset formulation to simplicial and topological message passing (Bodnar et al., 2021; Eijkelboom et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024a). 

# 7 Conclusion 

CSNN combines Clifford multivector stalks with a sheaf Laplacian, built from normalized reversion sandwiches and positive semidefinite by construction, with no versor constraint. Characterizing the sandwich family shows that these maps span exactly half of End R, expressivity grows with the number of terms, and CSNN’s reversion member is the first-order, single-ratio-conditioned corner of the family. A structural caveat concerns the sheaf-Laplacian kernel, where transported configurations agree and the relational signal vanishes. Since CSNN only approximates diffusion, this bounds the linear core rather than the model.
