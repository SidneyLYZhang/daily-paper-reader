## ABSTRACT 

Universal multimodal retrieval requires compact embeddings that preserve task-relevant semantic information across diverse modalities. Prior works have incor-porated latent reasoning into multimodal embedding learning to refine this infor-mation before embedding extraction. However, most existing approaches remain confined to deterministic latent paths, without exploring alternative trajectories to discover better embeddings. Thus, we propose VaME (Va riational Multimodal 

Embeddings ), a framework that models latent reasoning as a learnable distribu-tion over trajectories. Specifically, we first introduce Variational Latent Reasoning (VLR) to enable autoregressive exploration in latent space, guided by answer re-construction through a lightweight decoder. Meanwhile, we augment the original embedding-token readout with a latent-fused embedding to facilitate exploration during subsequent reinforcement learning. Finally, we optimize latent reason-ing over stochastic variational trajectories through reinforcement learning, using Semantic Decoding Reward (SDR) to favor semantically meaningful trajectories with interpretable decoded outcomes. On the 78-task MMEB-V2 benchmark, spanning image, video, and visual-document retrieval, VaME outperforms most explicit CoT-based models and all latent-reasoning baselines. VaME also demon-strates robust performance on reasoning-intensive benchmarks such as MRMR, with substantial gains after reinforcement learning. Importantly, VaME achieves these gains with at least a 4.25 × inference speedup over the deterministic latent autoregressive baselines. The code will be made publicly available. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Universal Multimodal Embedding (UME) aims to map text, images, videos, visual documents, and their mixtures into a shared embedding space, allowing a single model to serve diverse retrieval tasks (Radford et al., 2021; Wei et al., 2023). Recent vision-language embedding models (Liu et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025) have made substantial progress (Jiang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025b) by learning instruction-aware representations (Lin et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2024) across these modali-ties (Meng et al., 2026; Lan et al., 2025a). Yet universal retrieval is not simply a matter of compress-ing more modalities into one vector (Xiao et al., 2026). Depending on the instruction, the model must identify and compose task-relevant evidence into a compact representation. Accordingly, a growing line of work augments embedding formation with intermediate reasoning. Explicit approaches make this computation human-readable through retrieval-oriented rewrites (Wu et al., 2026a; Wang et al., 2026) or textual reasoning traces (Lan et al., 2025b; Jiang et al., 2026; Cui et al., 2025). However, the required autoregressive generation before embedding extraction (Cui et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2026) is far slower than direct embedding extraction, making these ap-proaches impractical for large-scale, real-time applications. Latent reasoning offers a compact al-ternative (Shen et al., 2025; He et al., 2026): methods such as LaME (Wu et al., 2026b) and TTE-Flash (Cheng et al., 2026) perform additional computation in hidden space while preserving an ef-ficient retrieval interface. Most latent approaches, however, remain deterministic (Hao et al., 2025; Shen et al., 2025; He et al., 2026): a given input follows one fixed computational path. Conse-quently, deterministic latent reasoning cannot fully benefit from exploration-based learning. This gap raises our central question: Can an embedding model explore diverse latent reasoning paths while retaining efficient inference? 

To answer this question, we propose VaME (Va riational Multimodal Embeddings), which learns a distribution over latent reasoning trajectories (Figure 1). Variational Latent Reasoning (VLR) replaces deterministic transitions with conditional Gaussians (Kingma & Welling, 2013; Rezende et al., 2014). Each autoregressive step feeds a sampled continuous latent action back into the back-bone, changing the context for subsequent actions (Hao et al., 2025; Shen et al., 2025). During la-tent supervised fine-tuning, retrieval objectives align trajectories with relevance, while a lightweight decoder reconstructs answer annotations from their hidden states (Yu et al., 2025). Alongside vari-ational regularization (Alemi et al., 2017; Li et al., 2025), this supervision helps preserve retrieval-relevant semantics without intermediate chain-of-thought supervision. To balance exploration and retrieval stability, we retain the original embedding-token readout for sta-ble single-vector retrieval and add a complementary latent-fused embedding for trajectory-sensitive retrieval supervision and reinforcement learning. In the second stage, we sample multiple varia-tional trajectories (Tan et al., 2025) and refine the continuous latent policy through group-relative optimization (Shao et al., 2024). Retrieval feedback rewards discriminative trajectories (Wu et al., 2026a; Jiang et al., 2026), while Semantic Decoding Reward (SDR) evaluates whether the frozen lightweight decoder produces answers semantically consistent with the reference. SDR discourages semantically empty exploration and makes latent reasoning outcomes inspectable through decoding. At inference, VaME follows one mean trajectory and emits one embedding per item, without text generation or trajectory search. We evaluate VaME on broad and reasoning-intensive multimodal retrieval benchmarks. Across the 78 tasks of MMEB-V2 (Meng et al., 2026), covering image, video, and visual document retrieval, VaME outperforms most explicit CoT-based models (Lan et al., 2025b; Cui et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2026) and all comparable prior latent-reasoning baselines (Wu et al., 2026b; Cheng et al., 2026; He et al., 2026). It further demonstrates robust performance on reasoning-intensive benchmarks such as MRMR (Zhang et al., 2025a), with substantial gains after reinforcement learning. Importantly, VaME achieves these gains with at least a 4.25 × inference speedup over the deterministic latent autoregressive baselines. Our contributions are as follows: • We introduce Variational Latent Reasoning, which formulates multimodal latent reason-ing as an autoregressive distribution over continuous trajectories, enabling training-time exploration with deterministic single-trajectory inference. • We develop a two-stage framework in which supervised fine-tuning aligns variational tra-jectories with retrieval relevance and answer semantics, and reinforcement learning then refines the latent policy using retrieval feedback and semantic decoding reward. 2• Extensive experiments on MMEB-V2 and MRMR demonstrate that VaME delivers strong retrieval performance across diverse multimodal retrieval tasks while achieving substan-tially faster inference than deterministic latent autoregressive baselines. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Universal multimodal embeddings. Prior works such as VLM2Vec (Jiang et al., 2025) and GME (Zhang et al., 2025b) train instruction-aware representations (Jiang et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2026c; Gu et al., 2026) with vision–language backbones (Wang et al., 2024; Lan et al., 2025a). MM-Embed (Lin et al., 2025) studies universal retrieval (Wei et al., 2023; Gu et al., 2025) across multimodal inputs (Jian et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025), while VLM2Vec-V2 (Meng et al., 2026) extends evaluation and training to video and visual document tasks. MetaEmbed (Xiao et al., 2026) explores flexible late interaction (Santhanam et al., 2022) and the allocation of mul-tiple vectors at test time. Our focus is complementary: we change the computation that precedes extraction while retaining a single-vector retrieval interface. 

Thinking before embedding. A growing line of work introduces an intermediate reasoning stage before deriving the final retrieval embedding (Shen et al., 2025; Deng et al., 2023). Some methods enrich the context for embedding extraction through textual CoT (Wu et al., 2026a; Wang et al., 2026; Lan et al., 2025b; Jiang et al., 2026), but require multi-step autoregressive decoding. Other methods process learned tokens in a single prefill pass (Wu et al., 2026b; Cheng et al., 2026) or autoregressively feed hidden states back into the backbone (He et al., 2026; Cheng et al., 2026). CoT generation can further supervise these latent representations (Cheng et al., 2026). These methods rely on explicit rewrites or deterministic latent computation. We instead use a stochastic policy over vector transitions, allowing the model to explore different latent reasoning paths. 

Continuous reasoning and variational representations. Continuous reasoning shifts intermediate computation from text to hidden states. Coconut (Hao et al., 2025) feeds hidden states back recur-rently, while CoDI (Shen et al., 2025), CoLaR (Tan et al., 2025), and SoftCoT (Xu et al., 2025a;b) learn or compress reasoning in continuous space. LaSER (Jin et al., 2026) and PLUME (He et al., 2026) apply latent reasoning to dense and multimodal retrieval. In parallel, variational autoencoders model reparameterized latent variables (Kingma & Welling, 2013; Rezende et al., 2014), while information-bottleneck methods learn stochastic representations (Tishby et al., 2000; Alemi et al., 2017), including multimodal and contrastive variants (Wu et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025). Our method seeks to introduce exploratory latent reasoning for multimodal retrieval.

## 4 VARIATIONAL MULTIMODAL EMBEDDINGS 

As shown in Figure 2, VaME establishes a two-stage training framework for learning multimodal embeddings through latent computation. Its central challenge is not merely to introduce noise or stochasticity, but to make sampled trajectories represent distinct computations, align their variation with retrieval, and confine exploration to training. Accordingly, Section 4.1 formulates autoregres-sive computation as a regularized conditional trajectory distribution; Section 4.2 aligns its samples with retrieval relevance and answer semantics through latent SFT and complementary readouts; Section 4.3 compares alternative trajectories using retrieval feedback and SDR, then improves the latent policy with group-relative RL. At inference, the learned policy follows its conditional means, retaining deterministic single-vector retrieval. 44.1 VARIATIONAL LATENT REASONING 

Stochastic latent recurrence. The latent recurrence in Equation 2 enables input-dependent refine-ment, but maps each input to only one deterministic trajectory. This leaves no alternative compu-tations for exploration or outcome-based comparison. We therefore introduce stochasticity into the recurrent transitions, allowing multiple latent trajectories to be sampled from the same multimodal input. VLR realizes this idea by replacing each deterministic transition with a conditional diagonal Gaussian. As shown in Figure 2, we introduce two special tokens, <LAT> and </LAT> , to mark the start and end of latent reasoning, respectively. Given an input x, a multimodal prefill produces an initial cache c0 and hidden state h0. Evaluation. We evaluate broad multimodal retrieval on MMEB-V2 (Meng et al., 2026), which has 36 image and 18 video datasets covering retrieval, classification, moment retrieval, and visual QA, all evaluated with Hit@1. Its 24 VisDoc datasets involve document images such as complex charts and paper figures and use nDCG@5. To assess reasoning-intensive retrieval, we also evaluate on MRMR (Zhang et al., 2025a), which comprises 11 expert-level tasks. We report Hit@1 for Negation and nDCG@10 for the remaining tasks, following the official MRMR protocol. 

Baselines. We compare VaME with representative MLLM-based multimodal embedders span-ning three paradigms: direct-embedding models such as VLM2Vec, GME, and Ops-MM-Embed; explicit-reasoning models TWN and UME-R1; and latent-reasoning models PLUME, TTE-Flash, and LaME. Most baselines follow the training and evaluation protocols of VLM2Vec-V2 (Meng et al., 2026), making their performance broadly comparable. We note that Ops-MM-Embed, GME, and LamRA use additional training sources, while UME-R1 and TWN uses CoT from external reasoners to enhance its embeddings. 5.2 MAIN RESULTS 

Results on MMEB-V2. As shown in Table 1, VaME-2B and VaME-7B score 65.7 and 69.4 on the 78-task MMEB-V2 benchmark, leading their respective size groups. At 2B, VaME outperforms LaME and PLUME by 1.3 and 4.1 points; relative to LaME, it gains 0.2 on Image, 1.8 on Video, and 2.3 on VisDoc. It also surpasses UME-R1 by 6.0 points at 2B and the explicit-reasoning baselines TWN and UME-R1 by 0.7 and 4.9 points at 7B, respectively. Thus, variational latent reasoning can improve multimodal embeddings across modalities without generated reasoning text.

## 7 CONCLUSION 

We present VaME, a variational multimodal embedding framework that models latent reasoning as autoregressive trajectory distributions. Latent SFT grounds trajectories in retrieval relevance and answer semantics, while Latent RL refines them with retrieval feedback and SDR. At inference, one conditional-mean trajectory yields a single embedding without textual reasoning or search. Results on MMEB-V2 and MRMR show that VaME outperforms prior methods in both broad and reasoning-intensive retrieval with substantially faster inference. Future work will explore adaptive computation to further reduce overhead and align stochastic training with deterministic inference. 9AI USE STATEMENT
