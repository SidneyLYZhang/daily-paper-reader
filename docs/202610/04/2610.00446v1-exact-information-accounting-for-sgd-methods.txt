# Exact information accounting for SGD methods 

## Akshay Balsubramani 

akshay@vac.bio 

Abstract 

As an alternative to the standard geometric analyses, we give an exact, information-theoretic analysis of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) and its variants. We show that a preconditioned SGD step is the posterior-mean update of a Gaussian Bayes model, and that its one-step regret splits into an intrinsic-time cost and a change in comparator information. The split extends to an identity for the objective itself. Convex convergence, strict-saddle-point escape, the link between flatness and generalization, the standard learning-rate schedules, adaptive optimizers, and the noisy, momentum, heavy-tailed, and gradient-free variants of SGD each correspond to a term or a special case of this identity. We measure its terms on synthetic and real training runs. On real networks it attributes the slack of classical convergence bounds to the terms dropped by their derivations and separates optimizers that reach the same training loss. That separation follows the number and consistency of their steps. Its relation to which of them generalizes better differs between networks. For gradient-free SGD the identity determines how a curvature preconditioner should enter the update. The sharpness-based generalization certificate it yields, with a data-independent isotropic prior, is vacuous at network scale unless the curvature spectrum is nearly flat across all parameters.

Scope and roadmap. The information ledger holds exactly for the local gradient score and the SGD mean recursion. The objective-level identities hold once the Bregman remainder is retained. Conventional rates still require assumptions, which enter visibly and locally where terms of the identity are replaced by envelopes. §4 builds the information ledger, from which §5 and §6 read off the classical theory. §7 measures its terms on real training runs and on problems where a single term has a closed form. The appendices hold full derivations, experimental protocols, and deferred proofs. 

# 2 Related work 

As the most widely used training algorithm in modern machine learning, SGD has a correspondingly vast literature. This section gives a representative overview of the parts closest to the present analysis. Classical and modern stochastic approximation analyzes SGD through expected gradient alignment, variance, and averaging [9, 72, 87], in the online-gradient-descent vocabulary of [37, 105]. In the information ledger the gradient alignment equals the change in comparator information plus the intrinsic-time cost (§4.2). Gradient variance enters through the stochastic-gradient intrinsic time (§5.3). Averaging enters through the weighted average identity (§5.1). The high-probability analysis of the last iterate under non-smoothness is sharpened in [36]. For unconstrained smooth convex objectives with sub-Gaussian gradient noise, [57] characterize the time-uniform rates attainable by the last iterate of SGD under deterministic step-size schedules fixed before the run. A boundary of order (log T )p/√T that holds at all iterations simultaneously with high probability, uniformly over that class, is attainable if and only if p > 1/2. Anytime-valid confidence sequences make time-uniform guarantees observable: [3, 4] certify the suboptimality and, under strong convexity, the last-iterate distance of projected SGD uniformly over time, stitching time-uniform envelopes [42] over an accumulated conditional-variance clock that they also call intrinsic time. Their certified envelopes relax the one-step identity from which §5.1 derives the contraction. The information ledger names the slack they retain: the curvature gain beyond the strong-convexity quadratic, the terminal information, and the certified envelope standing in for the true distance. 2Existing derivations of optimizers from Bayesian objects approximate the posterior, recovering SGD and AdamW as limits of Bayesian filtering on the gradients [1] or a family from ridge regression to RMSProp as instances of one Bayesian learning rule [49]. They are exact only in the limit of that approximation. Others add noise so that iterates approach posterior samples [101], tune constant-step SGD so that its stationary law approximates a posterior [66], or approximate the realized update by diffusion limits and modified equations [7, 56]. Safe Bayes [32] tempers the likelihood with an exponent η ∈ (0 , 1] to repair consistency under misspecification. The inverse temperature ηt of §4.1 plays that role. An exact correspondence does precede ours for one member of the family: an extended Kalman filter estimating a static parameter under exponential-family observation noise is algebraically identical, step for step, to online stochastic natural gradient on the log-likelihood [77]. That equivalence is specific to the Fisher-preconditioned update and couples the step to the filter’s adaptively contracting covariance. The present identity holds the covariance fixed, applies to any preconditioned first-order step, and is an equality on the realized trajectory with no approximation step, so its terms are measured. SGD’s stationary distribution admits a variational reading [11]: the steady-state law minimizes a free energy whose potential coincides with the training loss only when the mini-batch gradient noise is isotropic. That statement concerns the stationary distribution, in continuous time; the identity here is per step, on the realized discrete path. The terms of the information ledger meet several further literatures. Mutual-information and conditional-mutual-information bounds control the generalization of adaptive algorithms [75, 88, 96, 103], and [39] survey the resulting body of information-theoretic and PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds. The closest of these to the present accounting [75] advances a Gaussian-perturbed copy of the SGD path using gradients taken at the unperturbed iterates. That recursion is an instance of the noisy update accounted for in Appendix F, whose pathwise identity is stated for an arbitrary realized gradient and perturbation. The gradient need not be taken at the iterate advanced by the recursion. The mini-batch term of the expected intrinsic-time density is half the local gradient variance of that analysis, taken in the metric Σ and referred to the conditional gradient mean, identified in its discussion as the more natural choice. This paper is complementary in scope: it supplies the optimization-side information ledger. Its terminal information IT +1 (u) is the relative entropy between two Gaussians of covariance Σ, centered at the final iterate and at the comparator, which is the complexity of a PAC-Bayes bound whose prior is the second. The sharpness literature runs from [41] through the large-batch link [48] and the edge of stability of full-batch gradient descent [17], with [25] showing that many parameter-space flatness notions are not reparameterization-invariant. PAC-Bayes bounds make the flatness link explicit through Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities without explicit dependence on predictor dimension [34]. Local-entropy objectives bias optimization toward wide valleys [12]. In stochastic convex optimization, flat empirical minima can incur Ω(1) population risk while sharp minima of the same problems generalize optimally [90]. Sharpness-aware minimization and its metric variants [29, 50, 53] sit inside the identity as choices of ascent direction (Appendix E). The informationally natural sharpness is the log-determinant of the local Hessian (§6.1). Gradient methods avoid or escape strict saddle points under perturbation and noise [20, 28, 30, 44, 54]. The contribution here is the local saddle-point mechanism expressed as information amplification plus noise injection (§5.7). No sharper escape rate is claimed. RMSProp, Adam, momentum and acceleration, variance reduction, natural gradient, K-FAC, Shampoo, gradient clipping, parameter-free SGD, and optimistic online learning are each a specific reading of the same information ledger (Table 1, §5.3, and Appendices B and G). Cyclical, super-convergence, warm-restart, and warmup-stable-decay schedules [61, 94, 95, 102] redistribute when intrinsic-time costs are paid and when comparator information is transported. At large-language-model scale, realized loss curves under constant schedules with linear cooldown track a suboptimality bound from nonsmooth convex theory closely enough that optimal learning rates transfer across schedules [89]. Optimizers introduced since 2023 [15, 23, 46, 60, 100] choose different summaries of the realized gradient stream, each a reading of the information ledger through its choice of preconditioner Σt, forecast mt, and schedule ηt (Table 1, §B.2). Shampoo’s second-moment estimate has itself been recast as covariance estimation under relative-entropy minimization [59]. Choosing the preconditioner to minimize expected intrinsic time reduces to that estimation problem once the entropy of the prior is subtracted (§5.4).

## 6.3 Noise and momentum as entries of the information ledger 

Adding noise or momentum to SGD changes nothing structural in the information ledger: each attaches as one explicit term. The derivations are in Appendix F, where Table 2 lists the correspondences. Mini-batch score noise, post-step Gaussian injection, and Langevin heat contribute one additive quadratic entry each. The expected ledger depends on the perturbation only through its covariance, but the three attach at different places and scale differently in ηt. Finite-cumulant non-Gaussian noise replaces the Gaussian quadratic by the cumulant-generating function Λt(v) (Proposition F.8), of which it is the second-order term. Heavy-tailed and α-stable noise preserves the pathwise and channel relative-entropy identities, while the conditional-expectation entry is finite only after truncation, robustification, or a fractional/jump regime. State-dependent preconditioning adds a metric-drift term with an Itô correction. Phase-space momentum resolves into conservative Hamiltonian transport plus an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck thermostat channel, with heavy-ball a linear phase-space recursion (Proposition F.11) and Nesterov a lookahead-transport correction.

## 7.5 Limits 

The intrinsic-time fraction separates trajectories by the number and consistency of their steps, but its rank correlation with the train–test gap across optimizers is +0 .41 on ResNet-20 and −0.18 on the language model (Appendix H.2). The 18 intrinsic time accumulated over the first fifth of a run, net of metric drift, ranks the optimizers’ final training losses at a mean Spearman correlation of +0 .11 , positive on the language model and negative on ResNet-20, while the training loss at the same prefix reaches +0 .78 (Appendix H.2). As an index of loss decay, the intrinsic clock gives the same forecasts as the iteration count wherever VT grows as a power of T . Along every real-data run measured, VT grows nearly as a power of T (Appendix H.4). Across optimizers the bare Hessian statistics move under a function-preserving rescaling by as much as the distance between optimizers, while function-space curvature does not move. On ResNet-20 the correlation of function-space curvature with the gap has the implied sign at batch 32 and the opposite sign at batch 8192 , where 50 

epochs leave the runs far from fitting their training sets (Appendix H.6). A terminal certificate built on a data-independent isotropic prior is vacuous at network scale unless the curvature spectrum is nearly flat across all parameters, since its minimum over the prior scale grows with the spread of that spectrum (§6.2). On grokking and a vision transformer the pressure-cycled controller slows or degrades training, since its patience trigger fires only after the network fits its training set or while the training loss is still falling (Appendix H.5). 

# 8 Discussion 

The identity makes SGD analysis modular: any rate, any loss, any predictable side information, and any comparator produce one instance of the same accounting. It does not circumvent lower bounds: the Ω(1 /√T ) lower bound for convex stochastic optimization, lower bounds for gradient oracles, and minimax lower bounds for non-convex smooth optimization all still hold. The identity changes the structural position of known upper bounds and step-size rules: an upper bound becomes an identity plus explicit, quantifiable slack, and an adaptive step size becomes a controller driven by the intrinsic clock. The work closest to this accounting was offered as a first step [75], with its quantities referred to a population gradient whose meaning blurs across multiple passes, and with no account of why an SGD trajectory would hold those quantities small. An exact accounting removes the first difficulty by construction: it records the realized update’s own spending, and its expected intrinsic-time density refers the mini-batch term to the conditional gradient mean preferred in that work. The second stands: the identity accounts exactly for the information accrued along a trajectory but supplies no mechanism that keeps that information small along the trajectories followed by SGD. For SGD with label noise a partial mechanism is known: the algorithm converges to stationary points of the training loss plus an explicit penalty on sharp minimizers [19], and near a manifold of minimizers its limiting dynamics are a gradient flow of the Hessian trace along the manifold [58]. The Gaussian-Bayes construction is local. It uses the observed stochastic gradient as a local score and gives an identity for the resulting mean recursion. It does not assert that an arbitrary network’s data likelihood is globally Gaussian in parameters. The proper likelihood of Proposition A.1 is local as well: it observes a constructed Gaussian variable at each round and reheats the covariance between rounds. The objective-level identity is exact only when the Bregman remainder is retained. Without the remainder, the linearized loss bounds ft(θt) − ft(θ∗) under convexity (§5.1). For a nonconvex objective, smoothness with a Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition bounds F (θt) − F ∗ through the intrinsic-time density (§5.7). Under the linearized loss the posterior covariance stays at Σ. The quadratic composite loss of Appendix E lets the precision accumulate curvature, with heavier bookkeeping. The prior Σ is a design parameter, so a reading is informative only for a 

Σt whose perturbations are functionally meaningful. The linearized posterior N(θt, Σ) is specified by its mean, so the accounting costs no more than the update itself. Each empirical result built on the accounting holds within the boundary located for it in §7.5. SGD is the Gaussian-prior linearized instance of Bayes-rule updating. The identity accounts for the information accrued by this updating along each realized trajectory. The results treated here follow from the one-step identity by Gaussian bookkeeping (§5, §6), with intrinsic time as their common clock. 19 Appendices to “Exact information accounting for SGD methods”

## E.2 Coordinate dependence and practical estimation 

Under a change of parameters θ′ with Jacobian J = ∂θ ′/∂θ , the Fisher matrix transforms as F′ = J−⊤ FJ−1 at every point, and the Hessian transforms the same way at a critical point, or everywhere when the map is linear. Their log-determinants therefore shift by −2 log | det J|, a shift that differs between basins unless the map is linear. Nor is 

log det( I + βτ 2H) invariant. The relative entropy KL( q∥π) is invariant when posterior and prior are transformed together. At a critical point the spectrum of F−1H is also invariant, since it transforms by similarity. The trace and the spectral radius of the Hessian have neither property [25]. On a deep network only a truncated spectrum is computable, typically the top-K Ritz values returned by Lanczos with K ≪ d, cross-checked with a Hutchinson trace. Truncation adds failure modes of its own. A function-preserving rescaling moves which eigenvalues fall in the top-K subspace. Clamping the negative-curvature tail to a floor ε adds 

− log ε per floored eigenvalue, which can dominate the estimate as K grows. The long tail of small positive eigenvalues is undercounted, the Hutchinson trace and the sum of top-K Ritz values disagreeing by a residual that does not shrink reliably with K. The Fisher matrix is positive semidefinite, so the floor fires only on its near-zero Ritz values, far less often than on the Hessian’s.
