## ABSTRACT 

Methods for generalization in model-based reinforcement learning typically as-sume that an agent cannot recover the latent context governing the environment dynamics from its own experience, and therefore supplies it externally. We for-malize and test this assumption with predictive sufficiency , which quantifies what access to the context adds to next-step prediction under the visitation distribution an agent induces, and separates that quantity into a history-recoverable part, a residual requiring the true context, and the deficit added by a finite model. We classify context-aware algorithms by the predictive risk their conditioning set can target and demonstrate across environments of increasing identification difficulty that the headroom does not follow the MDP class. The same task under differ-ent priors leaves predictive headroom in one setting and nothing distinguishable from zero in another, where the agent’s behavior implicitly identifies the context and any benefit of such a mechanism cannot be attributed to missing information. Where headroom persists, the learned state exposes it only partially, and adding the true context still lowers the risk. Our contribution is a practical criterion for matching contextual mechanisms to the information available to them, estimated from the ordinary trained agent without a reference policy. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

In model-based reinforcement learning (RL), even minor changes in environment dynamics, e.g., the mass of a body or the friction of a floor in robotics, can degrade next-state predictions and, through compounding rollout errors, the policies built upon them (Lee et al., 2020). Settings with such variations are often formalized as contextual MDPs (Hallak et al., 2015) or hidden param-eter MDPs (Doshi-Velez & Konidaris, 2016). The latent parameters may be drawn i.i.d. from a shared prior (Benjamins et al., 2023), evolve according to a background process, as in dynamic parameter MDPs (Xie et al., 2021), or vary without structure, folding the problem into a general partially observable MDP. They can also change between episodes, during an episode, or even at every timestep (Dulac-Arnold et al., 2021). In these settings, a predictor that only sees the current state and action has to average over the contexts that are still plausible at that point, resulting in a model mixture and reduced prediction accuracy. A common solution is to infer the missing information online and provide it to the agent, where existing approaches include supervised system identification, meta-learned adaptation, latent context encoders, or conditioning on ground-truth context (Duan et al., 2016; Nagabandi et al., 2019; Lee et al., 2020; Zintgraf et al., 2020; Evans et al., 2022; R¨ oder et al., 2025; Prasanna et al., 1arXiv:2610.06651v1 [cs.LG] 5 Oct 2026 Preprint. Those methods aim for improving how a finite model uses experience without adding new ob-servations. However, the agent’s trajectories can contain sufficient evidence about the active context, and a model that uses history already has access to this information. What a contextual mechanism can still add is exactly the one-step advantage that observing the context grants a predictor under the data distribution the agent itself induces. Figure 1 illustrates these effects. Pooling distinct contexts yields an averaged kernel that governs no particular episode, while trajectories reveal or conceal the context depending on where they lead, identifying the dynamics before any dedicated mechanism infers them. We formalize this question through predictive sufficiency , quantifying the Bayes one-step advantage of context for memoryless and history-aware predictors. We distinguish prior-averaged structural variation from predictive headroom , the potential improvement in next-state prediction from ob-serving the true context under the agent’s observed trajectories. We split headroom into information recoverable from history and a residual requiring context, while separating the learned predictor’s finite-model deficit. Reading context-aware methods through the information they access turns the decomposition into class-level predictions: an encoder has no missing information to supply where visitation alone makes the context identifiable, short windows can recover headroom carried by re-cent evidence, and persistent representations can exploit information spanning episode boundaries. Our contribution is threefold: i) we analyze visitation-mediated implicit identification, where the agent’s behavior concentrates the posterior over the context until nothing is left for another mecha-nism to supply; ii) we formulate predictive sufficiency, which asks a representation to preserve the transition information the context carries rather than the context itself, and decompose the resulting gap exactly across the inputs a method conditions on; and iii) we propose a principled criterion, confirmed in experiments across MDP classes of increasing identification difficulty, that determines which conditioning sets have predictive information to add and whether linear decoding of a learned state recovers it. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

MDPs with latent context variables. Contextual MDP formulation (Hallak et al., 2015) also ap-pears as hidden-parameter (Doshi-Velez & Konidaris, 2016), latent (Kwon et al., 2021), and is closely related to Bayes-adaptive (Duff, 2002) MDPs, which consider a belief over the unknown pa-rameters. Work in these settings has studied exploration (Henaff et al., 2023), adaptation, regret, and zero-shot generalization (Kirk et al., 2023). Common benchmarks in gaming settings (Chevalier-Boisvert et al., 2023; Cobbe et al., 2020; Hafner, 2022) typically vary per-episode initial state dis-tribution rather than dynamics, while meta-RL tasks (Rakelly et al., 2019) mix reward, goal, and 2Preprint. Under review dynamics variation. The Real-World Reinforcement Learning suite (Dulac-Arnold et al., 2021) in-cludes dynamics randomization, stochastic disturbances, and degradations in continuous control, varied between episodes. CARL (Benjamins et al., 2023) turns continuous environment parameters into explicit context variables and defines protocols on contextualized control tasks. LILAC (Xie et al., 2021) instead considers structured evolution of an episode-level latent across a sequence of tasks. We use these settings to study transition prediction and ask whether and how predictive dif-ferences carry to downstream control. 

Context inference and conditioning. In meta-RL, PEARL (Rakelly et al., 2019) infers a latent task variable with a permutation-invariant encoder and VariBAD (Zintgraf et al., 2020) maintains a Bayes-adaptive belief over task parameters, separating information shared across tasks from infor-mation inferred within one, in the spirit of the empirical-Bayes recasting of meta-learning (Grant et al., 2018). For dynamics, UP-OSI (Yu et al., 2017) learns a supervised map from recent state– action histories to physical parameters that condition a universal policy, GrBAL and ReBAL (Naga-bandi et al., 2019) meta-train forward models that adapt from recent trajectory segments through gradient-based and recurrent updates respectively, and CaDM (Lee et al., 2020) and IIDA (Evans et al., 2022) infer a latent dynamics context from recent transitions without observing the true pa-rameters. Within Dreamer, cRSSM (Prasanna et al., 2024) conditions the world model and policy on observed context, while DALI (R¨ oder et al., 2025) infers it with a separate history encoder and forward-dynamics objective. While these methods offer inductive biases for inference from expe-rience, we ask a prior question, namely what information their inputs can supply under a given visitation, which our framework makes precise. 

## 3 THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK 

Preliminaries. We formalize the environment as a contextual MDP (S, A, T θ , R θ , γ ), where both the transition kernel Tθ and the reward function Rθ are parameterized by a latent context variable θ.We denote an episode trajectory by τ = ( s0, a 0, s 1, a 1, . . . , s T ) and its prefix up to time t by τ≤t.The context is drawn at the start of each episode, either independently from a prior p(θ | α) with shared hyperparameters α, or from an autonomous Markov chain p(θi+1 | θi) across episodes. We restrict attention to contexts fixed within an episode: time-varying context requires an augmented latent process and remains outside the present framework. Our analysis is concerned with the tran-sition kernel, and its predictive quantities require no reward labels.

## 4 CONTROLLED DIAGNOSTIC 

The framework distinguishes variation in a transition family from predictive headroom under the distribution generated by an agent. We test this distinction in tractable i.i.d. settings where the context-conditional kernels are known. MiniGrid (Chevalier-Boisvert et al., 2023) provides a finite state space in which both Istruct and Iemp can be evaluated from the exact kernels at the logged continuous contexts. The Point-Mass-Attractor environment provides a continuous-state counterpart with known noisy one-step kernels. 

Diagnostic design. We implement two MiniGrid transition variants: MiniGrid-Wind samples a drift that changes the realized displacement after each action, while MiniGrid-Resistance samples an action-failure probability. Point-Mass-Attractor (Figure 3, Appendix B) samples one of the attractor locations, causing trajectories to curve toward the selected center. In all cases, the diagnostic varies only an unobserved episode-level transition parameter. We collect trajectories under uniform random actions and record context for estimation while withholding it from the predictive information set. Wind and Point-Mass-Attractor have strongly separated kernels, but their induced states leave little one-step headroom, whereas MiniGrid-Resistance reaches similar headroom because its ker-nels differ little. 

## 5 EXPERIMENTAL VALIDATION 

Prior work has proposed a range of mechanisms for latent context MDPs, each targeting a specific level in a hierarchy of settings and covering the levels below it. Based on the theoretical analysis, we predict that predictive sufficiency under a given visitation determines when a mechanism has additional information to exploit: headroom must remain, and conditioning on its inputs must lower the corresponding optimal risk. The resulting bounds separate the available information from how effectively the inductive bias uses it, which depends on the information structure rather than the class the mechanism was designed for. Based on those insights, we formulate four falsifiable predictions: • (P1) Implicit identification renders observed context redundant for one-step prediction irrespective of the MDP class. When visitation makes the context identifiable from the current state and action, the agent’s own behavior has already performed the identification that context mechanisms are built for, making the predictive headroom vanish. Benefits of the mechanism therefore cannot be attributed to missing predictive information. • (P2) Predictive sufficiency separates information failure from finite-model error. When head-room persists but the episode’s history reaches the observed-context risk, any remaining predictive deficit for a model with access to that history is due to estimation, approximation, or optimization. • (P3) The sufficiency optimum is relative to its conditioning set. For methods that do not observe context, only mechanisms that carry information across episode boundaries can improve on the 6Preprint. Environments. Common benchmarks often vary initial states rather than the latent dynamics, while meta-RL suites mix reward with dynamics variation, and perturbation studies confound predictive headroom with task difficulty. To address this, we build on CARL (Benjamins et al., 2023) and RWRL (Dulac-Arnold et al., 2021), which isolate dynamics variation. As with the methods, we select representative environments for the classes of contextual MDPs studied in prior work, ordered by the difficulty of identifying the context. The two i.i.d. settings are based on DMC Walker from the CARL suite, repeating the contrast of Section 4 at benchmark scale: (i) an identifiable setting, where a latent horizontal wind, sampled uniformly per episode, exerts force through nonzero fluid density, probe headroom collapses while decodability stays high, and return remains competitive (P1, P4), and (ii) a hard setting, where a latent actuator-strength scale is sampled uniformly under fixed gravity and headroom persists above the permutation null (P2). The settings separate collapsed and persistent headroom under baseline visitation. Each method is then profiled under its own. In the non-i.i.d. drift setting, a single Walker parameter follows an RWRL perturbation schedule, performing a random walk across episodes at three rates on identical frozen realizations (P3). Additionally, we include a simple linear-Gaussian point mass environment whose hidden context evolves across episodes as a Markov chain with high self-transition probability, so completed episodes carry predictive value about the current one. There, known transition kernels and dis-turbance filters provide analytical references for calibrating the Gaussian-score diagnostics (Ap-pendix D). A short-episode variant limits within-episode evidence, and an independent-draw variant controls for the information supplied by cross-episode disturbance history (P3). One difficulty class remains uncovered: to our knowledge, no standard benchmark makes the context identifiable only through deliberately informative actions, so every passive class is predicted equivalent there. ## 6 RESULTS 

Settings with i.i.d. contexts. Table 4 confirms the intended contrast across selected methods. In the hard setting, estimated headroom remains open and varies for every method, while returns stay within 2% of the baseline. There, successful control coexists with unresolved predictive con-text. Residual context gain remains positive for the inference-based methods and vanishes only for cRSSM-D, whose linear readout carries the ground-truth context (P4). ## 7 DISCUSSION 

The results demonstrate that the value of context mechanisms is governed by the information struc-ture of the setting in addition to their design, consistent with the theoretical analysis. First, the probes tracked exact targets on the calibration setting, where history recovery reached 0.89 against the exact 

0.99 (P2), providing a reference for interpreting their estimates. Next, in the identifiable i.i.d. set-ting, the headroom collapsed under every profiled visitation while the context stayed decodable, so return differences reflect optimization rather than information. In the hard setting, estimated head-room stayed open across the evaluated methods despite comparable returns, showing that successful control does not have to resolve predictive context. Finally, the non-i.i.d. results connect predictive benefits to both the drift regime and mechanism design. The contrast between chain-latent infer-ence and carried state demonstrates the distinction between predicting episode-level context and preserving recurrent state, consistent with Equation 9. Our findings carry two implications: i) when Bayes predictive headroom is zero, any predictive gain under the same visitation reflects a reduction in finite-model error, and ii) for benchmark construc-tion, the class of a setting alone does not determine its difficulty. The same task yielded headroom well above the null in one setting but not in another, which suggests reporting estimated headroom alongside an MDP’s class and its benchmark definition. 

Limitations. In the present analysis all estimated quantities are properties of a closed loop: each method’s visitation shapes the information available to it, and that information shapes the method in turn. We deliberately do not compare under a shared reference policy, since no policy is neutral, random behavior collapses the visited region and competent behavior encodes the context it has al-ready identified, so any fixed choice would characterize that policy rather than the learning problem. The policy dependence is instead the mechanism under study: implicit identification is produced by the agent’s own behavior, and removing it would remove the phenomenon. It is also the condition-ing that matters in practice, since a deployed mechanism operates under the visitation its training produces, rather than under a standardized one. Extending comparison across visitations, through paired counterfactual resets where simulators per-mit them, and constructing settings whose context yields only to deliberately informative actions, are the future directions we consider most fruitful. 9Preprint. ## 8 CONCLUSION 

Motivated by real-world control, where the dynamics an agent faces vary and are rarely observed directly, a growing number of methods target MDPs with latent dynamics, but nothing tells a prac-titioner when any of them is needed: in practice the choice often follows the environment’s class and the method’s design intent. However, we show that both can be unreliable guides and propose a principled replacement. Our analysis establishes, and our experiments support, that the predictive opportunity available to a context mechanism is set by where the latent information resides under the trained agent’s visitation, yielding a criterion assessed from the ordinary agent alone. Behind it lies a simple observation: an agent that acts in a world also performs inference about it, and whatever its behavior identifies along the way is already available to the optimal predictor. ## B CONTROLLED DIAGNOSTIC DETAILS 

The controlled diagnostic uses two state spaces to test the same distinction. MiniGrid makes every 

(s, a ) pair enumerable, so Istruct can be evaluated directly and visitation can be manipulated by setting. Point-Mass-Attractor retains known one-step kernels in continuous state, where the empiri-cal quantity requires estimating only the occupancy-induced context posterior. MiniGrid-Resistance supplies a contrast with low Istruct within the discrete setting. 

Shared evaluation protocol. Context is sampled once per episode and omitted from the predictive information set, but retained in the logged data used to estimate conditional information. Because every context-conditional kernel is known in closed form, no next-state distribution is estimated from samples: empirical values score each logged transition’s exact kernel against a cross-fitted reference mixture, so every transition contributes and no count-based filtering occurs. All pooled transitions receive equal weight, while confidence intervals resample episodes. The standardized 

Istruct uses a fixed θ-independent query distribution. 

Discrete state: MiniGrid. Both MiniGrid families use the fully observed Markov state (x, y, d ) on the Empty-8x8 layout and leave the goal and reward unchanged. In MiniGrid-Wind, the episode context is v = ( vx, v y ) with independent coordinates sampled from U[−vmax , v max ]. After the native action, each axis receives drift sign( vj ) with probability |vj | and zero drift otherwise. Invalid displacement into a wall is blocked. MiniGrid-Resistance samples ϵ ∼ U [0 , ϵ max ]; an action takes effect with probability 1 − ϵ and otherwise leaves the state unchanged. Zero perturbation recovers the base environment in both families. For the standardized reference, μ is uniform over interior position, direction, and the seven Mini-Grid actions. The matched-query reference instead weights those same local calculations by (s, a )

frequencies in the collected trajectories. Figure 2 reports the full vmax and ϵmax sweeps. Each setting uses 5,000 uniform-random episodes of at most 256 steps. The Istruct calculation enu-merates ≈ 1,000 interior (s, a ) queries and integrates the continuous context prior by quadrature. The empirical estimator is a known-kernel occupancy plug-in: each logged transition contributes the exact Kullback–Leibler divergence between its context-conditional kernel and a kernel mix-ture over the transitions observed at the same (s, a ) cell. The reported value cross-fits the mixture leave-one-episode-out with one pseudo-count of uniform smoothing on the cell’s outcome support, which makes it a conservative upper estimate of the true headroom in expectation; the within-sample 14 Preprint. Under review mixture value is always within 2% of it. Percentile CIs come from 200 episode-level bootstrap re-samples with full re-estimation of all mixtures. The sweeps use vmax ∈ { 0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0}

and ϵmax ∈ { 0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9}.

Continuous state: Point-Mass-Attractor. Point-Mass-Attractor is a unit-mass two-dimensional system integrated at ∆t = 0 .05 . The hidden attractor is selected uniformly from the four corners 

(±1.5, ±1.5) and held fixed for the episode. At each step, the agent applies at ∈ [−1, 1] 2 and the attractor contributes force −k(pt −θ). Independent Euler–Maruyama increments add variance σ2∆t

to each velocity coordinate and σ2∆t3 to each position coordinate. We use k = 0 .3 and σ = 0 .1 for the representative setting. Episodes begin with p0 ∼ U [−0.5, 0.5] 2 and v0 = 0, and run for 50 steps (Figure 4). Positions are confined to [−2.5, 2.5] 2 by elastic reflection with velocity damping 0.5. Away from the boundary, the conditional kernel is diagonal Gaussian, with four means separated by a query-independent context displacement. Thus, the uniform and matched-query values of Istruct in the interior coincide exactly. Per coordinate, the context sign defines a binary-input Gaussian channel, so Istruct can be computed by one-dimensional Gauss–Hermite quadrature. A standard bound shows that boundary reflection has a negligible effect at the occupancies used here. Each k setting uses 5,000 episodes, with k ∈ { 0.05 , 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5}. The empirical estimate scores every logged transition’s exact context-conditional Gaussian kernel, including the exact single-reflection boundary densities, against the four-kernel mixture weighted by a cross-fitted k-NN context posterior (episode-level folds; the number of neighbors is selected by cross-validated log loss). For each candidate neighbor count the fitted mixture is a valid state-measurable predic-tor, so the estimate is biased upward in expectation up to a small model-selection effect. Percentile CIs come from 1,000 episode-level bootstrap resamples of per-episode contributions. As an inter-nal certificate, transitions immediately after a zero-velocity reset in the intervention datasets below have an exactly known uniform context posterior; the fitted estimator reproduces the exact stratum contribution within about 1.5% (for example, 0.4845 versus 0.4834 at reset interval two). 

Locating the visitation effect. Two checks vary the information carried by the current state. First, state projection removes coordinates from the conditioning variable while retaining the full next state. MiniGrid uses (x, y, d ), direction alone, or no state; Point-Mass-Attractor uses (p, v), po-sition, a 5 × 5 position bin, or no state. Coarsening therefore changes the estimand. The Mini-Grid projections are evaluated at a declared discretization of ten bins per context coordinate, with both the projected conditional and marginal formed as occupancy-weighted exact-kernel mixtures, cross-fitted leave-one-episode-out. The Point-Mass-Attractor projections use a cross-fitted classifier contrast: with four known context values, the estimand equals the difference between the context entropy given the projected state and given the projected state, action, and next state, each estimated by a cross-fitted k-NN class posterior (for the no-state projection the first term is the exact design prior ln 4 , making the estimate conservative). Every projection continues to condition on action. Second, resets intervene on visitation while preserving the episode context. MiniGrid resets position and direction before the current state is recorded, at intervals of ten, five, or two steps. Point-Mass-Attractor resets position at intervals of 25, ten, five, or two steps. Its control either resets velocity to zero or preserves it across the position reset. All subsequent transitions are recorded from the post-reset state. The projection results, depicted in Figure 5, locate context information already present in the ob-served state. Removing position from MiniGrid raises estimated Iemp by about 2,300%. In Point-Mass-Attractor, progressively removing velocity and position raises it by about 980%. The resets partially restore headroom without imposing the standardized query distribution. Relative to base-line, estimated MiniGrid headroom increases by 430%, 658%, and 833% as the reset interval de-creases. In Point-Mass-Attractor, resetting both position and velocity raises headroom by as much as 698%, increasing monotonically as the reset interval shrinks, whereas preserving velocity keeps it within 35% of baseline.

To this end, we calibrate the measurements using the simplest such system.

Calibration results. The estimated score gap in Table 7 tracks the exact target at a stable ratio in the primary and independent-draw settings, which characterizes its attenuation on the calibration envi-ronment. The unadjusted reference upper-bounds the population context gain for moment-matched diagonal-Gaussian predictors conditioned on state and action, by the argument above. The shuffled null stays near zero, while history recovery reaches 0.89 ± 0.03 of the 0.99 reference computed from the exact disturbance-history filter, giving a recovered fraction for the nested disturbance-only information sets rather than a certified bound on the full on-policy history optimum. The estimated sufficiency gap is near zero for the observed-context agent, concentrates at episode start for every in-ference method, and is largest for carried state, whose recurrent state continues the previous episode through the reset. The chain classes have no advantage at episode start, consistent with the small cross-episode increment in the closed form. The short-episode variant yields an exact per-transition residual of 0.24 ; since the scoring window covers all twelve steps of this variant, start-window and full-episode averages coincide there, and the probes reproduce the residual as a sufficiency gap that persists for the context-blind model and nearly vanishes for the observed-context one. Under the independent-draw control the exact cross-episode increment is zero and the chain classes remain indistinguishable from within-episode ones, consistent with P3. The characterized ratio reproduces at 0.51 ± 0.04 .These controls support using the probes as comparative diagnostics at the operating episode length; the attenuation ratio is characterized on this environment rather than assumed to transfer elsewhere.
