Abstract 

The theoretical understanding of multi-layer neural networks is largely confined to overpa-rameterized settings, which obscure parameter identifiability and incur high sample complexity. Neural tangent kernel (NTK) provides a general theory for wide networks, but does not offer efficient sample-complexity guarantees. Recent feature-learning results go beyond kernel methods for single-neuron, multi-index, and hierarchical targets. However, the analysis is often restricted to shallow or specific architectures and to the overparameterized regime. We break this paradigm to achieve parameter-level recovery of deep target networks, albeit by using active data queries. Specifically, we study L-layer polynomial networks with even degree-k monomial activations and nonnegative higher-layer weights. This structure makes the target network input-convex, while the optimization landscape remains highly nonconvex with respect to the parameters. Leveraging input convexity and active queries, we propose ASPIRE (Active Sam Pling for 

Iterative Recovery via Eigendirections), a layerwise sampling-based diagonalization algorithm that recovers all network parameters to δ-accuracy with sample complexity eOk,L

# 1 Introduction 

Deep neural networks have achieved remarkable empirical success, yet their theoretical understanding remains incomplete, in part because training is highly nonconvex. A major line of theory studies the overparameterized regime, where this difficulty becomes more tractable. In particular, the neural tangent kernel (NTK) framework shows that sufficiently wide networks can be approximated by kernel methods, yielding analyzable training dynamics and provable guarantees [Jacot et al., 2018, Lee et al., 2019, Jiang et al., 2025, Guillen et al., 2026]. However, kernel approximations largely suppress feature learning and can therefore fail to exploit the hierarchical structure of deep networks [Yang and Hu, 2021]. Beyond the kernel regime, recent theory has established provable learning of low-dimensional and hierarchical features [Damian et al., 2022, Wang et al., 2024b, Fu et al., 2025, Ren et al., 2026a]. However, these analyses are often restricted to shallow or specific network architectures and to the overparameterized regime. Under overparameterization, the model may lose the exact structure of the target, often leading to high sample complexity by ignoring inherent structures, as well as a lack of robustness under diverse environments. This makes exact-parameterization learning particularly appealing. However, there 1arXiv:2610.04999v1 [cs.LG] 4 Oct 2026 are technical barriers. Existing works typically can only explore the low-dimensional structures in the first or second layer, whereas the structures in the remaining layers cannot be explored. This is because that the input distributions for the rest layers are often much more complex (see more discussion in Section 3.2) and the core technique, Stein’s Lemma [Stein, 1981, Yang et al., 2017] cannot be applyied. Thus, it remains widely open how to learn multi-layer networks under exact parameterization, even when the activation function is polynomial and the input data distribution is designed. In recent years, the dominant success of large language models has witnessed the effectiveness of using useful data for training, as data quality, selection, and organization can substantially affect model training and downstream performance [Gu et al., 2025, Peng et al., 2025, Sachdeva et al., 2026, Subramanyam et al., 2026], with data access itself treated as part of the learning algorithm. Theoretically, it would be of great interest to provide separation results demonstrating the efficiency of data for neural network training. We study a strong oracle model where the learner can query the target function at arbitrary inputs. We further consider the target drawn from a depth-L

polynomial network with non-increasing widths, monomial activations, and nonnegative higher-layer weights. These assumptions endow the target network with a convex structure [Amos et al., 2017], allowing us to explore the strength of data. Input convex networks have been extensively used in scientific problems, where neural networks provide a flexible alternative to traditional methods for approximating operators. Input-convex neural networks offer a principled way to approximate convex functions, with wide-ranging applications spanning optimal transport [Makkuva et al., 2020], single-cell perturbation modeling [Bunne et al., 2023], model predictive control [Wang et al., 2024a], and power-system reliability [Christianson et al., 2025]. The input convexity does not make the training problem convex [Amos et al., 2017], it preserves the core optimization difficulty of deep learning. Instead, we propose a layerwise sampling-based diagonalization algorithm that succesfully recovers all parameters to δ-accuracy with sample complex-ity eOk,L

The main idea of our proposed algorithm is to construct two second-order moment matrices from active samples drawn uniformly from chosen convex sublevel sets. Under this sampling distribution, the even structure of the network induces coordinatewise sign symmetry, which diagonalizes the corresponding latent moments and allows the joint spectral structure of the two matrices to reveal the hidden directions. This enables layerwise recovery through spectral decomposition, with a two-Hessian variant for the final hidden layer and output weights. We further establish finite-sample error bounds and validate the theory numerically. As a result, our theory breaks the doubly exponential -in-depth sample-complexity barrier of the overparameterized paradigm, reducing the exponent from kL−1 to L2 + O(L). It also demonstrates that exact parameter recovery of certain deep networks is provably achievable under active quesries and provides the first justification for the effectiveness of using high-quality data in neural network training with an exponential separation. 22 Related Work 

NTK and Feature Learning. Foundational work established the NTK and lazy-training perspec-tive for sufficiently wide neural networks [Jacot et al., 2018, Lee et al., 2019, Arora et al., 2019, Du et al., 2019, Allen-Zhu et al., 2019, Chizat et al., 2019]. Subsequent work has further developed this framework by studying initialization, kernel evolution, and finite-width corrections [Chen et al., 2024, Jiang et al., 2025, Guillen et al., 2026]. Beyond this regime, provable feature learning encompasses single-neuron and multi-index models [Yehudai and Shamir, 2020, Wu, 2022, Damian et al., 2022, Mousavi-Hosseini et al., 2023], as well as nonlinear and hierarchical features in deeper compositional models [Nichani et al., 2023, Wang et al., 2024b, Fu et al., 2025, Tabanelli et al., 2026, Ren et al., 2026a,b]. These results primarily concern prediction, representations, or hidden subspaces. We take the next step toward estimating the target network itself: our algorithm is able to recover all parameters to arbitrarily small error. 

Parameter Recovery in Neural Networks. Prior work establishes parameter identifiability up to inherent symmetries for deep neural networks under suitable conditions [Fefferman, 1994, Bona-Pellissier et al., 2023]. Turning these structural guarantees into provably efficient reconstruction algorithms, however, is substantially more demanding. For ReLU networks, Rolnick and Körding [2020] exploit the geometry of activation boundaries to reconstruct architectures and parameters, although their analysis does not establish provably efficient end-to-end recovery. For polynomial networks, tensor-decomposition arguments establish generic finite identifiability of deep architectures under suitable conditions [Usevich et al., 2025]. Related work provides efficient black-box recon-struction of sums of powers of low-degree polynomials under suitable degree and non-degeneracy conditions [Garg et al., 2020]. For deep polynomial networks, existing results either establish identifiability without an efficient recovery procedure, or efficiently recover polynomial components without exploiting their shared hidden-layer structure. Our algorithm bridges both gaps by exploiting this shared structure iteratively: each recovered layer unlocks query access to the next, yielding end-to-end recovery of all network parameters with rigorous complexity guarantees. 

Input-Convex Architectures and Sampling Methods. A standard way to guarantee input convexity is to impose architectural conditions such as convex nondecreasing activations and nonneg-ative higher-layer weights [Amos et al., 2017]. Such architectures have found applications in optimal transport, computational biology, chemical engineering, and energy systems [Makkuva et al., 2020, Bunne et al., 2023, Wang et al., 2024a, Christianson et al., 2025]. In our setting, convexity permits sampling from convex body using Hit-and-Run, whose polynomial-time mixing is classical [Lovász, 1999, Lovász and Vempala, 2006]. Recent analyses sharpen its guarantees through localization and Poincaré-based bounds [Chen and Eldan, 2026, Zhang, 2026, Kook and Vempala, 2026]. Our noisy Hit-and-Run analysis further makes this sampling step robust to the approximation errors introduced by prefix inversion, enabling its repeated use throughout parameter recovery.

Despite the structural assumptions above, two fundamental challenges remain: the optimization difficulties of direct gradient methods and the limitations of overparameterized approaches. 

Difficulties of Direct Gradient Methods. Although the target network is convex in its input, direct fitting in parameter space remains highly nonconvex. Overparameterized methods are not naturally suited to exact parameter recovery: while they may achieve good prediction, the enlarged parameterization obscures the underlying target parameters. Moreover, fixed-kernel methods face a severe sample-complexity barrier. Building on the lower bound of Ghorbani et al. [2021, Theorem 3] for rotationally invariant kernels, we show that the infinite-width NTK of our setting requires dkL−1−o(1) samples. 

Proposition 3.7 (Barrier for the NTK) . For every fixed η > 0, learning our target class with the infinite-width NTK to constant Gaussian population error requires Ωk,L,η (dkL−1−η) samples. 

The proof, given in Appendix I.2, constructs an admissible target with a nonvanishing degree-kL−1

spherical-harmonic component, applies the rotationally invariant kernel lower bound on the sphere, and transfers it to Gaussian inputs using homogeneity. Recent feature-learning methods improve this scaling by using low-dimensional or hierarchical structure [Damian et al., 2022, Wang et al., 2024b, Fu et al., 2025], but they do not remove the exponential dependence on depth in the exponent of d. In their analysis, by assuming a low-dimensional structure of the first layer, they extract the subspace of the first-layer weights, typically using Stein’s Lemma [Stein, 1981]. However, the lifted space loses information needed to identify the original hidden directions. As only the span of the weights can be discovered, one needs to overparameterize the weights in the first layer to guarantee approximation. This makes the input distributions for the subsequent layers complex, as the input often lies on a low-dimensional manifold where Stein’s Lemma can no longer be applied to further extract the span of the weights for the remaining dimensions.

Scope of the Conclusion. The hard targets are chosen before the data are sampled and satisfy all of the manuscript’s assumptions; no additional highest-degree energy assumption is imposed on the entire target class. The lower bound concerns fixed, sample-centered infinite-width NTK regression, including zero-initialized kernel gradient descent. It does not rule out passive feature-learning algorithms, data-dependent kernels, or active queries. The use of Theorem 3 also requires k, L to remain fixed as d → ∞ ; the exponent statement alone is not a claim of an Ω( dq) lower bound with no exponent slack.
