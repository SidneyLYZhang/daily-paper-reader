# A Stochastic Maximum Principle for Partially Observed Jump-Diffusion Systems with Counting-Process Observations 

Jie Xiong and Ying Yang ∗†‡ 

June 10, 2026 

Abstract 

This paper derives a stochastic maximum principle (SMP) for partially observed jump-diffusion systems whose observations are multivariate counting processes with state-dependent intensities. By introducing a reference probability measure under which the observation pro-cesses become unit Poisson processes, the partially observed control problem is rewritten as a fully observed control one on the conditional distribution, leading to a conditional Hamil-tonian stationarity condition as a necessary optimality principle. We apply the framework to a linear-quadratic financial execution problem. Matching coefficients yields a closed-form Riccati feedback law driven by the conditional mean of the latent state. Numerical experi-ments with a particle-filter implementation demonstrate that the resulting controller captures the non-Gaussian filtering structure arising from both asymmetric jumps and the nonlinear counting-process observation mechanism, improves filtering accuracy over Gaussian and jump-blind approximations, and achieves closed-loop performance close to the full-information oracle. 

Keywords: Stochastic maximum principle, partial observations, jump-diffusion processes, counting-process observations, linear-quadratic control, particle filter, financial execution. 

# 1 Introduction 

Partially observed stochastic control problems arise when the controller must act on the basis of an incomplete observation of the state process. In many applications, including high-frequency financial execution, queueing network control, and event-driven sensor management, the available information takes the form of random event arrivals rather than a continuous diffusion signal. These observations are naturally modeled by multivariate counting processes whose intensities depend on the latent state, leading to a partially observed control problem with counting-process observations. This observation structure is technically distinct from the classical Brownian setting. Under diffusion observations, the innovation process is a continuous martingale and the Zakai equation is driven by a Wiener integral. Under counting-process observations, information arrives only at discrete random times; the likelihood-ratio process that connects the physical and reference measures acquires jump terms of the form (λk(X(t−), t −) − 1)d( Yk(t) − t),and the corresponding

Consequently, in any variational analysis of the partially observed problem, both the state jumps and the observation jumps must be handled simultaneously. The stochastic maximum principle (SMP) for fully observed jump-diffusion systems is well established; see Tang and Li [1] for the convex-control case and Øksendal and Sulem [2] for forward– backward systems with jumps. For partially observed systems under diffusion observations, the SMP was developed by Tang [3] and extended to jump-diffusion state dynamics by Xiao [4] and Wang, Wu and Xiong [5]; see also [6–9] for related formulations and further references. On the filtering side, Zeng [10] formulated a partially observed micromovement model for as-set prices with counting-process observations and developed a Markov chain approximation for the associated filtering equations; Xiong and Zeng [11] subsequently established convergence of a branching particle approximation to the optimal filter. For applications of counting-process obser-vation models, Xiong, Zeng and Zhang [12] derived a pre-commitment solution of the mean–variance portfolio selection problem. More recently, Zheng and Shi [13] derived a partially observed SMP for a forward–backward system in which the observation equation contains a jump component; however, the jump intensity in their observation is independent of the latent state, so that the ob-servation carries no state-dependent information through the jump channel. In the present work, the counting-process intensities λk(X(t−), t −) depend explicitly on the latent state: each trade ar-rival conveys information about the unobserved price, and this state-dependence is precisely what generates the likelihood-ratio dynamics and the nonlinear filtering problem. This structural dif-ference necessitates the reference-measure transformation and the associated variational analysis developed below. To our knowledge, a stochastic maximum principle for partially observed systems with state-dependent counting-process observations has not been established. This paper addresses this gap. We consider a controlled jump-diffusion state process observed through multivariate counting pro-cesses with state-dependent intensities, and restrict the admissible controls to the observation filtration. By introducing a reference probability measure under which the observations become unit Poisson processes independent of the state, we transform the partially observed problem into a fully observed one with an augmented likelihood-ratio state. A variational analysis of the aug-mented system then yields a stochastic maximum principle expressed as a conditional Hamiltonian stationarity condition. The contributions are as follows. (i) We derive a necessary optimality condition in the form of a stochastic maximum principle (see Theorem 10) for partially observed systems in which the state follows a controlled jump-diffusion and the observation is a multivariate counting process. (ii) We apply the result to a partially observed linear-quadratic financial execution problem. For this LQ instance, the adjoint process admits a linear ansatz in the state and likelihood ratio. And matching coefficients yields a closed-form Riccati feedback law (equation (4.14)). We further provide a real-time algorithmic implementation based on a bootstrap particle ap-proximation of the counting-process filter, and validate the closed-loop performance through paired Monte Carlo experiments comparing the proposed controller with several benchmark schemes. The numerical experiments are not intended as an empirical trading backtest. Their purpose is to validate the closed-loop behavior, robustness to model misspecification, and computational feasibility of the feedback controller derived from the stochastic maximum principle. 2The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 formulates the partially observed control problem and introduces the reference probability measure. Section 3 establishes the stochas-tic maximum principle. Section 4 applies the theory to the financial execution problem, derives the Riccati-filtered feedback, and presents the numerical implementation. Section 5 concludes. Proofs of the technical lemmas are collected in the Appendix.

The controller remains robust across all three parameters. The largest sensitivity occurs for ϑ: under-estimating the mean-reversion speed by a factor of two increases the cost by about 5.3 × 10 −3, less than 1% of the nominal cost magnitude | ¯J| ≈ 0.61 . This is consistent with the dual role of ϑ in the Riccati gain (4.12) and the filter prediction step. In contrast, misspecifying σ produces only 10 −5-level changes in the closed-loop cost. The confidence intervals in the σ panel include zero at all misspecification levels, so the observed non-monotonicity is not statistically significant. 4.4 Economic and engineering interpretation 

The explicit feedback decomposition (4.14) admits a transparent market-microstructure interpre-tation by separating the strategy into three economic components. First, the filtered deviation term ̂ πt − ¯X represents the conditional estimate of how far the latent efficient price is from its long-run level. Under the mean-reverting dynamics, the decision-maker increases the control effort, corresponding to selling pressure under the sign convention of (4.1), when the filtered state is above ¯X, and takes the opposite action when it is below ¯X.Second, the deterministic terminal-target term eϑ(T −t)( ¯X − k1/2) accounts for the terminal tracking objective. Since X2

In particular, under the terminal tracking choice k1 = 2 ¯X,this term vanishes and the controller reduces to a filtered regulator around the long-run level ¯X.Finally, the overall responsiveness to these signals is modulated by the horizon-dependent gain 

ρP (t)/(2 k2). This multiplier increases as the remaining horizon shrinks and attains its terminal value ρ/k 2 as t ↑ T , reproducing the standard linear-quadratic intuition that control becomes more aggressive near the terminal time. Thus, the feedback law shares the linear-quadratic execution structure of the Almgren–Chriss framework [14], but differs in two important respects: the state entering the feedback is filtered from discrete-tick counting observations rather than directly observed, and the state dynamics allow jumps to represent abrupt information shocks. The reduction of the stochastic adjoint system to (4.14) therefore provides an implementable partial-information feedback law depending on the latent state only through its conditional mean. 26 5 Conclusion 

We derived a stochastic maximum principle for partially observed jump-diffusion systems with multivariate counting-process observations. By applying the result to a linear-quadratic financial execution problem, we obtained a Riccati-filtered feedback law in which the latent state enters the controller only through its conditional mean. The numerical experiments demonstrate that the particle-filter implementation improves filter-ing accuracy over a Gaussian-projection baseline under asymmetric jump distributions, and that this improvement can translate into a statistically significant cost reduction in the nominal setting. Additional experiments illustrate the effects of jump-aware filtering, model misspecification, and particle count on the closed-loop controller. Establishing sufficient optimality conditions for the partial-information setting, and extending the framework to non-Markovian or multi-asset observation models, are left for future work.
