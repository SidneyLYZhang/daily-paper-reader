# Tight Lower Bounds for Stochastic Nonconvex-Strongly-Concave Minimax Optimization 

Siqi Zhang ∗ Qilong Wu † Junchi Yang ‡

September 29, 2026 

Abstract 

We study the stochastic first-order oracle complexity of finding ϵ-stationary points of the primal function in smooth nonconvex-strongly-concave minimax optimization. For sufficiently small ϵ, we establish lower bounds of Ω( κL ∆σ2ϵ−4) under the bounded-variance assumption and Ω( κ3/2 ¯L∆σϵ −3) under the additional assumption of averaged smoothness. Here, L and ¯L denote the smoothness and averaged-smoothness constants, respectively, ∆ is the initial primal gap, 

σ2 bounds the oracle variance, and κ = L/μ or ¯L/μ in the respective settings, where μ is the strong-concavity parameter. Our bounded-variance lower bound improves the dependence on the condition number from κ1/3 in previous lower bounds to κ, while our averaged-smoothness lower bound is the first of its kind. In both settings, the resulting lower bounds match existing upper bounds in their dependence on κ and ϵ. Our proofs are based on a unified quadratic lifting construction that transfers a hardness instance for stochastic nonconvex minimization to unconstrained minimax optimization while preserving the required variance and smoothness properties.

# 2 Related Literature 

Lower Bounds for Minimax Optimization. First-order oracle lower bounds for convex-concave and strongly-convex-strongly-concave minimax problems were established by Ouyang and Xu (2021) and Zhang et al. (2022a), while Xie et al. (2020) studied the finite-sum setting. For nonconvex-strongly-concave (NC-SC) problems, Zhang et al. (2021) derived lower bounds for both the deterministic and averaged-smooth finite-sum settings. Han et al. (2024) further studied finite-sum NC-SC problems under an oracle model that allows access to both component gradients and proximal operators. Closest to our setting, Li et al. (2021) established deterministic and stochastic lower bounds for NC-SC problems; under bounded variance, their stochastic lower bound contains the term Ω( L∆κ1/3σ2ϵ−4). For a broader overview of oracle-complexity results in minimization and minimax optimization, we refer to Zhang and Hu (2025).

Recent Lower Bounds beyond Strong Concavity. Recent work has extended lower-bound analyses to minimax problems in which strong concavity is relaxed to concavity or the Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) condition. For NC-C problems with a compact dual domain, Wu et al. (2026) establish deterministic and stochastic lower bounds of Ω( ϵ−3) and Ω( ϵ−6), respectively, for zero-respecting algorithms under Moreau-envelope stationarity. Deterministic lower bounds for projected zero-respecting methods are also developed by Zhang and Xu (2026). Pan et al. (2026) establish the Ω( ϵ−3) deterministic lower bound for arbitrary first-order algorithms and provide a matching upper bound. For NC-PL problems, Pan and Li (2026) establish an Ω( L∆κϵ −2) lower bound for 4arbitrary deterministic first-order methods, matching the known upper bound in the corresponding parameter regime. In particular, their result demonstrates that relaxing strong concavity to the PL condition can lead to a stronger dependence on the condition number κ in the deterministic setting. In contrast, our work focuses on stochastic NC-SC problems and characterizes how the bounded-variance and averaged-smoothness oracle models determine the dependence on κ.

# 6 Conclusion and Future Work 

We establish stochastic first-order lower bounds for fully unconstrained NC-SC minimax optimization over zero-respecting algorithms. For sufficiently small ϵ, we prove lower bounds of Ω( L∆κσ 2ϵ−4)under bounded variance and Ω( L∆κ3/2σϵ −3) under averaged smoothness. Both results follow from a unified construction that couples a nonconvex zero-chain with a deterministic quadratic lifting, and both extend to Moreau envelope stationarity without worsening the dependence on κ. These lower bounds match recent stochastic upper bounds in terms of their dependence on κ and ϵ.Several questions remain open. First, it would be useful to extend the lower bounds beyond zero-respecting algorithms to more general, possibly randomized, first-order methods. Another direction is to better align the domain and initialization assumptions in upper and lower bounds. Finally, our zero-chain hard instances require a dimension that grows with the target accuracy; whether comparable lower bounds hold in fixed or low dimensions, in the spirit of Chewi et al. (2023), remains open.
