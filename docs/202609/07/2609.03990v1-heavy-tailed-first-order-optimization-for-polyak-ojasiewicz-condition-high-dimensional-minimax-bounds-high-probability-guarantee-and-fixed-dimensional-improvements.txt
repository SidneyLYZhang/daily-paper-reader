21 Introduction 

The Polyak–Łojasiewicz inequality provides one of the most useful intermediate models between strongly convex and fully nonconvex optimization. For a differentiable objective f , it requires 12 ∥∇ f (x)∥2 ≥ μ(f (x) − f ⋆),

without requiring f itself to be convex. Nevertheless, when f is also L-smooth, this inequality is sufficient for gradient descent to converge globally at a linear rate governed by the condition number κ = L/μ (Polyak (1963a); Karimi et al. (2016a)). The PL framework therefore captures a broad class of objectives that retain a global optimization guarantee even though their geometry may be nonconvex (Hardt and Ma (2016); Li et al. (2018); Liu et al. (2022); Allen-Zhu et al. (2019)). Understanding the precise oracle complexity of PL optimization is consequently important both for identifying the limits of first-order algorithms and for explaining which parts of strongly convex optimization theory continue to hold under the weaker gradient-dominance structure. In stochastic optimization, however, the geometry of the objective is only one source of difficulty. Classical analyses often assume bounded variance or sub-Gaussian gradient noise, while empirical studies of stochastic gradients in modern learning problems indicate that their distributions can be strongly non-Gaussian and heavy-tailed (Şimşekli et al. (2019)). A natural model assumes only conditional unbiasedness and a finite centered α-moment of stochastic gradient oracles Gt,

E[Gt | F t−1] = ∇f (xt), E[∥Gt − ∇ f (xt)∥α | F t−1] ≤ σα, 1 < α ≤ 2.

Under this assumption the variance may be infinite, empirical averages need not exhibit Gaussian concentration, and rare but arbitrarily large oracle errors cannot be neglected. Gradient clipping, normalization, and robust mean estimation have consequently become central tools for obtaining high-probability guarantees under heavy-tailed noise (Cutkosky and Mehta (2021a); Sadiev et al. (2023); Puchkin et al. (2024)). Yet clipping a stochastic gradient around the origin can introduce a bias depending on ∥∇ f (x)∥, which may be unbounded on an unbounded domain. This makes it nontrivial to obtain a high-probability theory that is simultaneously robust to heavy tails, compatible with mirror geometry, and free of bounded gradient or bounded domain assumptions. A second challenge concerns optimality. In the noiseless high-dimensional setting, smooth PL optimization has the lower-bound scale Ω( κ log(∆ 0/ϵ )), showing that the general PL class does not admit the √κ acceleration available under strong convexity (Yue et al. (2023a)). Stochastic lower bounds based on probabilistic zero-chains and random rotations provide a powerful way to hide sequential information from arbitrary first-order algorithms (Arjevani et al. (2023a)). For 

heavy-tailed noise, however, a complete lower bound must capture two different obstructions at once: the deterministic optimization transient and the statistical cost of estimating a mean under only a finite α-moment. Moreover, it should be noise-adaptive : when σ = 0, the stochastic term vanishes, but the lower bound should reduce to the correct exact-gradient complexity rather than becoming trivial. The role of dimension creates a third difficulty. Randomly rotated zero-chains require a dimension that grows with the number of hidden directions, whereas low-dimensional optimization may permit geometric search strategies unavailable in high dimension. Gradient-flow trapping, for example, exploits separating curves or hypersurfaces to locate approximate stationary points in fixed dimension (Bubeck and Mikulincer (2020a)). Extending this idea to the present stochastic setting is delicate because the algorithm has access only to noisy gradients, rather than exact function values on an entire separating surface. It is therefore not clear a priori whether the condition-number factor in the high-dimensional stochastic complexity remains unavoidable when d is fixed, or how the answer depends jointly on the dimension and the tail exponent. 3Motivated by these issues, this paper asks the following question: 

What is the optimal first-order complexity of smooth PL optimization as a joint function of κ, σ,

ϵ, the moment order α, and the dimension d, when the guarantee must hold with high probability and remain meaningful in the noiseless limit?

1.1 Related Work 

Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) Condition. The PL condition traces its origins to the seminal works of Polyak (1963b) and Lojasiewicz (1963). It is widely recognized as a relaxed struc-tural assumption of strong convexity and has been extensively applied to various optimization paradigms, including minimax problems Guo et al. (2020, 2023). Several other relaxations of strong convexity exist in the literature, such as error bounds (Luo and Tseng (1993)), restricted secant inequalities (Zhang and Yin (2013)), quadratic growth (Anitescu (2000)), essential strong convexity (Liu et al. (2014)), and weak strong convexity (Necoara et al. (2019)). For a com-prehensive comparison of these conditions, we refer readers to Guille-Escuret et al. (2021). In the deterministic regime, gradient descent achieves linear convergence for PL functions (Polyak (1963b)), and Yue et al. (2023b) recently established a tight lower bound for this setting. Karimi et al. (2016b) further extended the PL framework to constrained optimization by introducing the proximal PL condition. In the stochastic setting, Khaled and Richtárik (2020) and Li et al. (2021) independently established an expected complexity oracles of O(Lσ 2/μ 2ϵ) for finding an 

ϵ-optimal solution of L-smooth and μ-PL functions, where σ2 denotes the gradient noise variance. 

Heavy-tailed stochastic optimization. The study of stochastic optimization beyond the classical finite-variance or sub-Gaussian regime is motivated in part by empirical evidence that stochastic-gradient noise in modern learning systems can be highly non-Gaussian and exhibit heavy-tailed behavior Şimşekli et al. (2019). Early algorithmic work demonstrated that 7gradient clipping can restore high-probability guarantees for smooth convex and strongly convex optimization, including accelerated first-order methods, without imposing light-tail assumptions Gorbunov et al. (2020a). Meanwhile, plain SGD has also been shown to converge under more specialized state-dependent infinite-variance models for strongly convex objectives Wang et al. (2021). For stochastic convex optimization under only a finite moment strictly larger than one, Vural et al. Vural et al. (2022a) established minimax-optimal mirror-descent guarantees and matching information-theoretic lower bounds without explicit gradient clipping or normalization. In smooth nonconvex optimization, Cutkosky and Mehta Cutkosky and Mehta (2021a) combined clipping, momentum, and normalized gradient steps to obtain high-probability stationarity guarantees under bounded p-th moments, whereas Liu, Zhang, and Zhou Liu et al. (2023b) refined the generic analysis and showed that additional sample-function structure can permit rates faster than the corresponding unstructured oracle lower bound. Sadiev et al. Sadiev et al. (2023) subsequently developed a broad high-probability framework under bounded central α-moments covering smooth nonconvex, Polyak–Łojasiewicz, convex, and strongly convex optimization, as well as several classes of variational inequalities. Recent extensions include stochastic mirror descent with general Bregman geometry and generalized Bregman Prox-PL conditions Fatkhullin and He (2024), composite and distributed stochastic optimization based on clipping stochastic gradient differences Gorbunov et al. (2024), smoothed median-of-means procedures that exploit additional structure in the heavy-tailed noise distribution Puchkin et al. (2024), and clipped adaptive methods such as AdaGrad-Norm and Adam-Norm Chezhegov et al. (2025). More recently, Fradin et al. Fradin et al. (2026) established matching lower and upper bounds for broad classes of stochastic nonconvex problems under generalized stochastic smoothness assumptions and developed double-clipped methods with high-probability guarantees. The present work complements this literature by focusing on noise-adaptive upper and lower bounds for smooth PL objectives under the minimal finite α-moment oracle model, while further identifying how the stochastic complexity changes between dimension-growing and prescribed fixed-dimensional regimes. 

Lower Bounds. Lower bounds for deterministic convex optimization were pioneered by Nesterov (2013), while Carmon et al. (2020) focused on finding ϵ-stationary points in non-convex settings. In the stochastic regime, Agarwal et al. (2009) and Raginsky and Rakhlin (2011) established fundamental limits for convex functions, results that were later extended to heavy-tailed noise settings by Vural et al. (2022b). For stochastic non-convex functions, Arjevani et al. (2023b) presented lower bounds for finding ϵ-stationary points. Recent work by Saad et al. (2025) utilized a novel divergence decomposition technique to provide lower bounds for non-convex functions with specific structures, such as the restricted secant inequality. Additionally, lower bounds have been explored in the finite-sum setting (Fang et al. (2018); Zhou and Gu (2019)) and for zeroth-order oracles (Duchi et al. (2015)). While Yue et al. (2023b) provided a tight lower bound for PL functions in the deterministic case and Bai et al. (2024) provided a lower bound for finite-sum setting under μ-PL and L-mean-squared smoothness, a tight lower bound for the stochastic PL setting has remained an open question. 

High-Probability Convergence. Early high-probability analysis for stochastic mirror descent under sub-Gaussian noise was provided by Nemirovski et al. (2009). Subsequent works by Gorbunov et al. (2020b, 2021) relaxed these noise assumptions to handle smooth and non-smooth settings, respectively. Beyond convexity, high-probability rates for SGD with momentum were established by Li and Orabona (2020), and later by Cutkosky and Mehta (2021b) using gradient clipping to handle heavy-tailed noise. Liu et al. (2023a) further explored high-probability rates within the stochastic mirror descent framework. Adaptive methods have also seen significant progress: Kavis et al. (2022); Attia and Koren (2023) provided analyses for AdaGrad, while Hong and Lin (2024) extended these guarantees to Adam. Recently, Liu et al. (2024) applied 8gradient clipping to non-convex, non-smooth problems to achieve high-probability convergence under heavy-tailed noise.

3.1 Main ideas and proof sketch 

The lower bound has two logically independent hard families. The first is the Bernoulli-thinned heavy-tailed chain developed in the next sections. The second is an exact-gradient geometrically weighted chain. Because an exact oracle has centered α-moment zero, it is legal for every announced noise level σ ≥ 0. The noise-adaptive lower bound is therefore at least the maximum of the two component risks, which gives the additive noise-adaptive statement. 

Stochastic component. The stochastic hard family starts from a smooth robust zero-chain of width ms = Θ( κ). If the current point has not reached coordinate k + 1, every later gradient coordinate vanishes. Bernoulli thinning returns the next coordinate only with probability qs

The proof has four independent ingredients. 1. A universal one-coordinate heavy-tail obstruction. A pair of shifted quadratics is coupled so that the two oracle laws are identical unless a rare event of probability q occurs. The centered α-moment is of order q1−α, while the separation between the minimizers is of order σq (α−1) /α /μ . Taking q ≍ (μϵ/σ 2)θα produces the sharp mean-estimation scale Qα(ϵ). Embedding the construction in the first coordinate works in every fixed dimension. 2. One-dimensional localization. For a one-dimensional PL function, the minimizer set is an interval, the derivative is negative to its left and positive to its right, and PL implies a quadratic-growth inequality. A robust estimate of the derivative sign therefore supports bisection. This removes every polynomial dependence on κ from the stochastic term. 3. Gradient-flow traps in dimensions at least two. A compact box whose boundary cannot be reached from its pivot while decreasing f faster than slope c must contain a point with gradient norm at most c. Two parallel cuts of the longest side shrink such a box by a constant volume factor. Approximate relative values on a fine net of the new cut preserve the trap after paying only Od(τ /s + Lδ 2/s + cδ 2/s 2) in the slope parameter. 4. Potential reconstruction from stochastic gradients. Relative function values are line integrals of the gradient. A stratified randomized quadrature on a segment of length h has deter-ministic error O(Lh 2/m 3/2) and heavy-tail error Oα(σh/m (α−1) /α ). A dyadic tree shares these line integrals among all points of a ( d − 1)-dimensional cut. The accumulated noise is summable exactly when pα ≥ d − 1; this is the source of the phase transition in (209). The remainder of this part proves each ingredient and then assembles the stagewise high-probability algorithm.
