# ABSTRACT 

Protein structure modeling rests on a single computational primitive: the inter-action between what a residue is (sequence content) and where it sits (three-dimensional geometry). What is the expressive limit of this layer class? We show that the complete bilinear operator over content–geometry outer products—the sufficient statistic of all second-order interactions—is the expressive ceiling, while the additive message passing of mainstream geometric GNNs is provably blind to content–geometry binding. We then introduce Hyper-Fold, a rank-K separable convolutional backbone approaching this ceiling at message-passing cost: each radius neighborhood is organized into a sequence hyperedge and a contact hyper-edge, modulated by an edge-conditioned matrix-valued operator factorized into 

K learned basis operators with geometry-generated coefficients. Across enzyme function prediction, fold classification, and ligand binding site detection, Hyper-Fold and its hierarchical variant Hyper-Fold-Deep achieve the best results among protein-specific structure encoders; Hyper-Fold-Pocket, an anchored set-prediction head, surpasses UniSite-3D on UniSite-DS and two zero-shot benchmarks with no sequence language model features, 68 × fewer parameters, and 4.8× lower latency— suggesting that a sufficiently expressive 3D backbone recovers information that fusion architectures previously borrowed from evolution-scale pretraining. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Protein function is determined by three-dimensional structure, and structure modeling is dominated by a single computational primitive: the interaction between what a residue is (its sequence/content features) and where it sits relative to its neighbors (three-dimensional geometry). Message-passing (MP) architectures (Jing et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2023b; Fan et al., 2023) implement this primitive by propagating geometry-aware messages over residue graphs and remain the most effective structure encoders at modest scale (Jamasb et al., 2024); protein language models and hybrid variants (Lin et al., 2023; Su et al., 2024; Hayes et al., 2025) fold structure into large-scale sequence pretraining. Yet beneath this diversity, no one has systematically answered a basic question: what is the expressive limit of sequence–geometry interaction layers, and where do existing models sit relative to that limit? We answer this question: any second-order statistic of a neighbor’s content and geometry is a linear functional of their outer product xj ⊗ ϕ(δij ), so the complete bilinear operator over this outer product is the sufficient statistic—and hence the expressive limit—of sequence–geometry interaction (Theorem 1). The result is an expressivity ladder (Figure 1(a)). Situating existing models on this ladder exposes a structural blind spot. We validate Hyper-Fold on three tasks that span the uses of protein structure encoders: enzyme commission (EC) number prediction, fold classification, and ligand binding site (pocket) detection. Our contributions are: (i) an expressivity ladder for sequence–geometry layers, with the complete bilinear operator characterized as its ceiling (Thm. 1) and additive message passing proved blind to content–geometry binding (Prop. 2); (ii) Hyper-Fold, a rank-K separable convolutional backbone that approaches this ceiling at message-passing cost; and (iii) experiments on three tasks, including state-of-the-art pocket detection and zero-shot generalization without sequence-model features, at essentially no extra efficiency cost. 2Under Review

Proposition 2 is not a corner case: additive layers cannot represent any function of how content and geometry are paired within a neighborhood—which residue type sits at which geometric position. Gated layers distinguish binding swaps with increasing channel structure—one direction shared by all channels (scalar), C directions that never mix (channel), full cross-channel interaction (matrix)—so the ladder is a hierarchy of how richly content and geometry may be bound. 

Theorem 1 (The bilinear ceiling) . Every layer whose message is a second-order (bilinear) function of a neighbor’s content xj and geometry features ϕ(δij ) factors through the outer product xj ⊗ ϕ(δij );conversely, class (5) realizes all such functions. Hence the complete bilinear operator is the sufficient statistic of second-order sequence–geometry interaction and constitutes the expressive ceiling of sequence–geometry layers.

Hyper-Fold consists of structure backbones built from a single convolutional operator, Fold-Conv (§3.2, Figure 2), instantiated in two variants (§3.3): Hyper-Fold preserves the sequence length and produces residue-level features, while Hyper-Fold-Deep interleaves residue pooling and produces protein-level features. For pocket detection we attach Hyper-Fold-Pocket, an anchored set-prediction head on the residue-level backbone (§3.4, Figure 3). 3.1 SEQUENCE AND CONTACT HYPEREDGES 

A residue relates to its neighborhood through two qualitatively different channels: sequence adjacency 

along the backbone chain and spatial contact in the folded state. Inspired by hypergraph modeling (Feng et al., 2019; Yadati et al., 2019)—where a hyperedge connects a center vertex to a set of related vertices—we organize the radius neighborhood N (i) = {j : ∥pi − pj ∥ < r } of each residue i into two hyperedges (Figure 2(a)): a sequence hyperedge over the sequence neighbors 

Nseq (i) = {j ∈ N (i) : |si − sj | ≤ ℓ/ 2} and a contact hyperedge over the remaining spatial-contact neighbors Ncon (i) = N (i) \ N seq (i), where si is the sequence index and ℓ the sequence-window width. We deliberately do not adopt hypergraph propagation rules; the hyperedge view only organizes the neighborhood, and both hyperedges are processed by the same matrix-gated convolution below, whose kernel network treats the two hyperedge types with separate embedding weights (§3.2). Hyper-Fold (residue-level). Six Fold-Conv blocks are stacked in two stages of three at a fixed width of 512 , with a wider sequence window and radius in the deeper stage (Figure 2(d), top; per-stage configurations in Appendix B). A two-scale readout taps the two stage outputs (blocks 3 and 6), each an RL×512 residue-level feature map that preserves the full sequence resolution. Pocket detection consumes both levels separately (§3.4), where every residue must be scored; classification mean-pools each level and concatenates the pooled vectors into a protein embedding in R1024 .

Hyper-Fold-Deep (protein-level). For global prediction tasks, Fold-Conv blocks are interleaved with residue pooling along a widening channel pyramid ( 256 → 512 → 1024 → 2048 channels; Appendix B), so the spatial resolution shrinks ( L → L/ 2 → L/ 4 → L/ 8) while the channel capacity grows (Figure 2(d), bottom); a final global pooling produces a protein-level embedding fprot ∈ R2048 

that feeds an MLP classifier for EC and fold classification. 3.4 HYPER -F OLD -P OCKET : ANCHORED SET PREDICTION FOR POCKETS 

Pocket detection is cast as set prediction (Carion et al., 2020): the model outputs a set of pockets, each a confidence score paired with a per-residue mask, without post-processing clustering. Hyper-Fold-Pocket builds on the residue-level Hyper-Fold backbone—every residue must be scored, so the pooling pyramid of Hyper-Fold-Deep does not apply—and follows a four-stage pipeline (Figure 3): a neck projects the two backbone levels into query, key, and memory features; a pocket proposal network (PPN) selects structure-anchored queries; a transformer decoder refines them over the memory; and two heads read out each pocket. Relative to the DETR paradigm, the design is decoder-only, anchors its queries on the actual 3-D structure, and predicts masks instead of boxes (detailed comparisons with DETR-style detectors and UniSite are in Appendix B). 

Structure-anchored proposals over a bi-level memory. Instead of learning the queries as free parameters, a linear pocketness probe over the fused residue features scores every residue, and the top-Nq (Nq =50 ) become proposals: their own residue features initialize the query content, and their Cα coordinates, embedded by a three-dimensional Fourier positional encoding (3D-PE), provide the query position—in the spirit of anchored query selection in RT-DETR (Lv et al., 2024), but with anchors that are actual residues. The decoder memory is likewise structure-preserving: the two backbone levels, tagged by learnable level embeddings, are stacked into a bi-level memory 

Memb ∈ R2L×256 , which a 4-layer transformer decoder cross-attends to directly—with no interposed transformer encoder—injecting the 3D-PE at every layer.

# A EXTENDED RELATED WORK 

Geometric deep learning on protein structures. Structure-based encoders represent a protein as a residue graph and learn with SE(3)-invariant or equivariant layers: GVP (Jing et al., 2021) introduced geometric vector perceptrons; GearNet (Zhang et al., 2023b) added edge message passing and remains a strong multi-task baseline (Jamasb et al., 2024); CDConv (Fan et al., 2023) and GCPNet (Morehead & Cheng, 2024) explore continuous or geometry-complete alternatives. §2 stratifies these layers by their gating algebra; here we only note the closest point in expressivity, CDConv, whose matrix-valued kernels match ours in principle but are memory-intensive at full residue resolution, forcing early downsampling that trades away exactly the residue-level detail tasks such as pocket detection require. Hyper-Fold keeps the matrix-gated kernel but factorizes it at rank 

K over sequence and contact hyperedges, bringing the cost into the message-passing regime at full sequence length— 4× fewer parameters and lower latency than CDConv (Table 1)—which is precisely what makes the residue-level Hyper-Fold-Pocket head practical. 

Sequence models and sequence–structure fusion. Protein language models pretrained at evolution scale (Rives et al., 2021; Lin et al., 2023) dominate sequence-based prediction, and hybrid models inject structure into them (Su et al., 2024; Hayes et al., 2025). Our results suggest that a sufficiently expressive 3D backbone can make such fusion unnecessary (§4.2). 12 Under Review 

Ligand binding site detection. Geometry methods (Fpocket (Le Guilloux et al., 2009)), ML rescoring (P2Rank (Krivák & Hoksza, 2018)), and CNN/GNN detectors (Aggarwal et al., 2021; Smith et al., 2024; Sestak et al., 2024) operate on surface or graph features; UniSite (Fan et al., 2025) reframed detection as set prediction over fused sequence–structure features. We adopt the set-prediction formulation but replace the encoder. 

Expressivity of graph networks. The WL hierarchy bounds standard MPNNs (Xu et al., 2019), and invariant tensor networks characterize maximal universal layers (Maron et al., 2019); on hypergraphs, the expressive power of hypergraph neural networks has been characterized via generalized Weisfeiler– Lehman tests (Feng et al., 2026). Our analysis is orthogonal and finer-grained: within the geometry-conditioned layers actually used on proteins, we stratify expressivity by the algebraic form of the content–geometry interaction, in the spirit of factorization-machine analyses of feature interaction (Guo et al., 2017). 

Hypergraph modeling. Hypergraph neural networks (Feng et al., 2019; Yadati et al., 2019) generalize pairwise edges to hyperedges that connect a vertex to a set of related vertices, with HGNN + (Gao et al., 2023) unifying vertex- and hyperedge-level propagation, and hypergraph isomorphism computation (Feng et al., 2024) characterizing structural equivalence of such high-order structures. Hypergraph modeling has also proven effective in detection: Hyper-YOLO (Feng et al., 2025) constructs hypergraphs over visual features to capture high-order correlations among candidate regions. Hyperedges are a natural language for the two residue groupings we use—sequence adjacency and spatial contact. Hyper-Fold borrows this grouping view only: each residue anchors a sequence hyperedge and a contact hyperedge, but information propagation is carried out by our own matrix-gated convolution (§3.2), not by hypergraph propagation rules.

Relation to DETR-style detectors. Hyper-Fold-Pocket borrows the set-prediction formulation of DETR (Carion et al., 2020) but redesigns every component for three-dimensional structures. (i) No transformer encoder : DETR-family detectors interpose a transformer encoder between the convolutional backbone and the decoder; we replace it with a lightweight projection neck over the two backbone levels, so the decoder cross-attends directly to a convolutional, geometry-aware memory. (ii) Structure-anchored queries : DETR’s queries are freely learned embeddings, and anchor-based variants (Liu et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023a; Lv et al., 2024) parameterize or select 2-D anchor boxes from encoder outputs; our proposals are anchored on actual residues—content initialized from the top-pocketness residue features, position from their C α coordinates—fixing both the query content and its 3-D reference point without any encoder. (iii) Masks instead of boxes : each proposal predicts a per-residue mask by inner product with the residue keys, rather than regressing a bounding box. (iv) Contrastive denoising : DN-DETR and DINO (Li et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023a) reconstruct noised ground truth as an auxiliary positive-only task; we additionally label heavily noised copies as 

negatives , teaching the decoder to reject near-miss decoys around real pockets. The bi-level memory plays the role of the multi-scale encoder features of Deformable-DETR (Zhu et al., 2021), but comes from two depths of a single convolutional backbone rather than an image pyramid. 

Relation to UniSite (Fan et al., 2025). UniSite-1D/3D instantiate the DETR template almost verbatim for pocket detection: a 6-layer transformer encoder processes fused ESM–GearNet-Edge residue features, and a 6-layer decoder refines 32 freely learned site queries (zero-initialized content with learned positional embeddings), read out by classification and inner-product mask heads and trained with Hungarian-matched BCE and Dice losses only. Hyper-Fold-Pocket shares the set-prediction formulation but differs in three ways. (i) Proposals are discovered, not learned : our queries are the top-Nq residues by pocketness, carrying the selecting residues’ own features as content and their C α coordinates as 3D-PE anchors, whereas UniSite’s queries are data-independent parameters that must localize pockets through cross-attention alone. (ii) No transformer encoder :UniSite interposes six encoder layers between the convolutional backbone and the decoder; our decoder acts directly on the bi-level convolutional memory, in which sequence–geometry interactions are already encoded by the backbone. (iii) Contrastive denoising : UniSite trains with matched losses only, while CDN adds positive reconstructions and near-miss negatives ( +1 .5 AP@0.5, Figure 7). A fourth, orthogonal difference is that Hyper-Fold-Pocket is purely structural: its residue features come from Hyper-Fold without any sequence model, whereas UniSite-3D leans on ESM features fused with GearNet-Edge. 15 Under Review

Remark 2 (The realizability gap vanishes with capacity) . Remark 3 (Sequence clamping) . The discretization bucket(∆ s) is exact for |∆s| < ℓ/ 2 (each integer offset owns a bucket) and merges all pairs with |∆s| ≥ ℓ/ 2 into the two boundary buckets.
