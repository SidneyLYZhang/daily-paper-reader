## Correlation flow governs learning at criticality 

Andrea Combette, Nelly Pustelnik, and Antoine Venaille 

CNRS, ENS de Lyon, Laboratoire de Physique UMR5672, Lyon, France 

The initialisation of deep neural networks determines whether information and gradients can propagate across depth, yet a unified theory connecting these properties to learning dynamics remains elusive. Combining mean-field theory and random matrix theory, we establish a direct link between correlation propagation and the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) that governs learning in the sequential limit of infinitely wide, infinitely deep networks. Correlation propagation to infinite depth is possible only at a single critical point in the weight-bias variance plane. At this point, we show that the end-to-end Jacobian vanishes algebraically with depth, and use this to prove that the NTK becomes exactly proportional to the output correlation at infinite depth. This equivalence between information propagation and learning dynamics had not yet been noticed. We further show that orthogonal initialisation suppresses the leading finite-size corrections present under Gaussian initialisation, clarifying the respective roles of the two initialisation ensembles in this limit. These theoretical predictions are validated quantitatively on finite-width, finite-depth networks. Together, these results demonstrate that orthogonal initialisation at criticality plays a central role in controlling the asymptotic dynamics of deep learning. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

Context. The remarkable empirical success of deep neural networks has been accompanied by an equally remarkable increase in their size [1, 2], raising concerns over the sustainability of continued scaling [3] while highlighting a fundamental theoretical question: what princi-ples govern signal propagation and trainability in such highly overparameterized systems? Modern architectures routinely contain hundreds of layers and billions of parameters, yet their successful optimization depends critically on how these parameters are initialised: poor initialisation causes information either to progressively vanish as signals and gradients propagate through the network, or to be amplified until they become unstable. Controlling this balance was the goal of the earliest initialisation schemes, which prescribe the weight variance so that activation and gradient statistics neither vanish nor explode across layers, ranging from the original heuristics of [4, 5] to the widely used Xavier and Kaiming initiali-sations [6, 7]. Understanding how initialisation governs trainability more broadly is one of the central theoretical problems of deep learning [8, 9]. 

Focus on correlation – For sufficiently wide networks, this question naturally becomes one of statistical mechanics: rather than following the microscopic dynamics of every neuron, one seeks a macroscopic description in terms of a small number of control parameters, such as the variances of the weights and biases [8, 10–12]. In the infinite-width limit, wide networks become Gaussian processes [13, 14]; throughout this paper, we refer to this limit, together with its associated Gaussian description, as the mean-field regime 1. Within this regime, macroscopic observables such as the network correlation obey deterministic recursion relations between layers, enabling a quantitative description of information propagation through arbitrarily deep random networks [18]. For generic initialisation parameters, this recursion converges exponentially fast with depth, so that information about distinct inputs is rapidly collapsed or amplified [19–21]. At a particular critical point , both variance and covariance converge algebraically, removing any finite characteristic depth scale and yielding universal, depth-independent propagation shared across broad families of activation functions [21]. This regime has motivated the view that initialisation at criticality is the best way to guarantee correlation propagation and trainability [8].

Focus on Jacobian – Trainability depends on gradients, not only on correlations, yet the two are intimately tied. As shown by [21], the correlation transition coincides with the boundary between vanishing and exploding gradients. They showed that the average singular value of the Jacobian, relating the pre-activations of one layer to the previous one, is exactly equal to the correlation susceptibility governing the local stability of this latter. However, this layer-wise control does not guarantee, on average, a proper behaviour of the end-to-end Jacobian [22]. Moreover, these approaches are restricted to the average singular value of the Jacobians, while [19, 23] argued that training also requires the end-to-end Jacobian to be well-behaved along every singular direction. This stronger requirement is captured by 

dynamical isometry – the regime in which the full spectrum of the end-to-end Jacobian singular values concentrates near unity, ensuring uniform gradient propagation across all directions. Orthogonal initialisation – drawing the weight matrices to be orthogonal rather than independently Gaussian – provides a natural mechanism to approach this property, allowing unitary singular-value spectra where Gaussian initialisation leaves them broad [19], analytically accessible through free probability theory [23, 24]. 

Focus on Neural Tangent Kernel – The quantity that most directly connects these initialisation-time properties to the training process itself is the Neural Tangent Ker-nel (NTK), the two-point correlation function of output gradients in parameter space [25]. In the infinite-width limit, this kernel stays constant throughout training, so gradient descent reduces to an implicit kernel regression whose asymptotic dynamics are entirely fixed by the NTK spectrum at initialisation: eigen-directions with large eigenvalues are learned quickly, those with small eigenvalues slowly or not at all [26]. This frozen or lazy regime contrasts with the feature-learning (rich) regime, in which the kernel itself evolves during training [17]. Which regime is preferable is architecture- and task-dependent [27, 28], but for fully con-nected networks the lazy regime is often competitive, without a principled explanation of why. 

The sequential limit hypothesis – At finite width, deviations from the mean-field Gaussian statistics accumulate with depth [29]. These deviations are governed by the depth-to-width ratio L/N [8, 18, 30]. Such fluctuations yield two, seemingly opposite, consequences [8, 31]. On the one hand, their uncontrolled growth with depth can lead to an ill-conditioned NTK. On the other hand, for small but nonzero L/N , these same finite-width corrections make possible a genuine feature-learning regime [8]. [32] further showed that orthogonal initialisation can suppress the uncontrolled growth and the resulting harmful divergence while preserving the learning-relevant corrections that enable feature learning. Fairly assessing this tension between the frozen and feature-learning regimes requires a complete characterization of the L/N → 0 baseline itself – the sequential limit studied here. In this context, correlation propagation, Jacobian spectra, and the NTK form three faces of the same problem. While quantitative relationships between them have begun to emerge [8, 33–35], how this threefold connection is conditioned by the choice of initialisation remains largely open. 

Contribution. In this work, we close this gap in the sequential limit , in which the width N

is taken to infinity before the depth L. We demonstrate that initialisation at criticality is the only possible choice for correlation propagation; we show that, despite this control, the end-to-end Jacobian nonetheless vanishes algebraically with depth, precluding dynamical isometry even under orthogonal initialisation; we characterize the finite-size fluctuations that arise around this limit, showing that orthogonal initialisation suppresses their leading O(L/N )contribution and thereby extends the range of applicability of the sequential limit baseline; and we unveil a previously unnoticed relation between the fixed-point correlation and the NTK itself, stemming from this same algebraic decay of the end-to-end gradients. Together, these results unify information propagation, gradient control, and learning dynamics within a single framework. To obtain these unified results, additional assumptions must be made about the neural network architecture, particularly regarding the activation function. These assumptions are mild and encompass the standard activation functions commonly used in 3Implicit Neural Representations [36] and Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) [37], such as sin and tanh , among many others [8].

> V. ALGEBRAIC BEHAVIOUR OF THE END-TO-END JACOBIAN AND ABSENCE OF DYNAMICAL ISOMETRY

Training a deep network relies on back-propagation, which requires the end-to-end Jacobian to remain stable as depth increases. At the layer-wise level, this requirement coincides exactly with covariance propagation as the mean of the layer-wise Jacobian spectrum equals the perpendicular susceptibility χ⊥ [21]. [22] showed that even if the mean approaches unity, the end-to-end Jacobian, which is obtained by accumulating it over depth, can still vanish algebraically. In addition, perpendicular susceptibility characterizes gradients only on average .To guarantee well-behaved gradients in every direction, the stronger condition of dynamical isometry , i.e., unit mean and vanishing variance of the full Jacobian spectrum, is needed. This was introduced by [23], who considered random matrix theory to bear on this question and identified orthogonal initialisation as the key to approaching dynamical isometry. Using this random-matrix framework, we re-derive the algebraic vanishing (generalizing [22] via an alternative method) and analyze spectral fluctuations of the end-to-end spectrum, beyond the first moment. This directly links gradient vanishing to absence of dynamical isometry and provides the groundwork methodology for the rest of this article. Throughout this section we work in the proportional scaling limit nout = O(N ), as intro-duced in Sec. II A: in this regime the end-to-end Jacobian retains a non-trivial eigenspectrum, with fluctuations that persist asymptotically. By contrast, the fixed output dimension limit 

nout = O(1), presented in Appendix D 1, yields a trivial spectrum with vanishing variance. 18

VI. THE NEED FOR ORTHOGONAL INITIALISATION TO CONTROL NTK EIGENVALUES 

In the previous sections, we analyzed correlation flow and the singular value distribution of the end-to-end Jacobian at criticality. We now leverage these results to characterize the training dynamics of the network, which, in the infinite-width limit, are fully determined by the eigenvalue spectrum of the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) at initialisation [25]. 23

This highlights the importance of a well-controlled NTK eigenspectrum for stable training, while the eigenvectors determine the learning directions .In practice, the learning rate λ must be chosen small enough to keep every mode stable, that is, λ < 2/μ max , with μmax the largest NTK eigenvalue. μmax is therefore not, by itself, problematic as it can always be absorbed by rescaling λ. How-ever, the spread of the NTK spectrum directly controls how many steps are needed to learn every direction in function space. Controlling this spread is thus essential for efficient training.

> VII. DISCUSSION

Summary. Let us unpack what the results above imply in practice, taking them in reverse order of their presentation. First, we established that the NTK governing learning dynamics is, throughout the critical regime studied here and at large depth, exactly proportional to the large-depth correlation ραβ  

> ∞

, with a proportionality constant fixed solely by the activation function (see (92)). Remarkably, this holds even though the end-to-end Jacobian itself vanishes algebraically and dynamical isometry fails, which turns out not to be an obstruction to predictable and controlled learning. Second, turning to finite-width corrections, we found that orthogonal initialisation suppresses the leading fluctuations that otherwise accumulate with depth under Gaussian initialisation (see (53) and (54)). Third, we showed that, at criticality, the limiting correlation is itself an explicit function of the initial corre-lation ραβ  

> 0

under the condition ϕ2 = 0 (see (39)) – depending strongly on input normalisation. 

Key takeaways. Taken together, our theoretical and numerical results identify orthogonal initialisation at the critical point as a principled choice for training very large and deep networks, substantially extending the depth over which learning dynamics remain predictable. 

Implications of correlation–NTK equivalence for learning. Our results demonstrate the practical relevance of similarity propagation through the network. Because the NTK is asymptotically proportional to the output correlation at criticality, the correlation we characterise is what determines the NTK spectrum and its eigen-directions. Whether a given similarity structure helps or hinders learning depends on the task. For classification, inputs sharing a label are typically similar in a well-chosen representation, and this proximity must survive propagation to remain informative: excessive decorrelation (chaotic regime, or the input-norm-driven decorrelation discussed above) scrambles same-label inputs, while excessive collapse (ordered regime) makes even differently labeled inputs indistinguishable. 32

For regression, the same similarity structure controls the regularity of the learned function, with collapse and decorrelation corresponding to over- and under-smoothing of the target, respectively. It is well established that input geometry can be decisive for performance, independently of the network’s bulk architecture: random feature maps approximating a task-relevant kernel improve classification accuracy without architectural adaptation [45], and Fourier or sinusoidal embeddings counteract spectral bias in regression settings such as implicit neural representations [36, 40]. Our results offer a first-principles account of why such representation-level interventions are effective within the NTK regime: at criticality, the network provably propagates whatever similarity structure is present in its input to its output, without needing to construct task-relevant structure itself – and, under orthogonal initialisation, this description remains accurate over a practical range of depths.

This gives a concrete, practical justification for input normalisation: anisotropy in input norms can induce an effectively chaotic loss of correlation even at criticality, and even a perfectly 33 isotropic dataset – distributed on a hypersphere Snin −1 – suffers a similar degradation once its radius becomes too large. This raises a natural question: if nonlinearity destroys correlation, why not favour near-linear networks? Likely because the same nonlinearity is what lets the network reshape, rather than transport, input similarity – a purely linear network preserves correlation perfectly, but cannot build task-relevant structure absent from the input. We leave a precise characterization of this trade-off to future work, which will be a key focus to understand, more precisely, what the features of the NTK are, outside the linearized regime in Sec. VI D. 

Connections to existing initialisation practice. As a concrete example, consider sine networks (SIREN [36]), commonly used to represent a signal, image, or physical field directly as a continuous function of its coordinates, as in physics-informed neural networks. The original SIREN architecture prescribes a weight variance corresponding to the chaotic 

regime identified in Sec. III B, while the default PyTorch scheme instead places the same network in the ordered regime (its bias variance, σ2 

> b

= 1 /N , sits asymptotically close to the vanishing-variance case, but remains in practice within the ordered phase). [39] recently proposed initializing such networks at criticality instead, reporting improved performance on a range of such tasks – but justified this choice only as one point along the broader edge of chaos line, without a specific argument for criticality itself. Here we provide this missing justification: criticality is the only choice that keeps the NTK controlled deep into the network while preserving information. 

Limitations and perspectives. Several of our simplifying assumptions deserve further investigation. We work throughout with a fixed, finite dataset, which places us in a strongly over-parameterized regime. Bringing the analysis closer to practical settings will require allowing the dataset size to scale sublinearly with the number of network parameters, as observed empirically [2]. In addition, for simplicity we restrict our study to fully connected networks. It would be interesting to investigate how the correlation–NTK relation generalizes to commonly used architectures such as MLPs with skip-connections [47], CNNs [48], and transformers, building on recent efforts toward a unified theoretical framework [8]. Beyond these extensions, it would be valuable to disentangle the respective roles of architectural design choices and initialisation schemes in determining network performance on a given task. We also restrict our derivation of finite-size corrections to the sequential limit itself, where L/N → 0; the joint limit, where L/N is small but constant as N diverges, has been thoroughly studied [8, 31] and is believed to underlie the onset of feature learning beyond the frozen-NTK regime. Our results suggest that this onset is itself initialisation-dependent, since orthogonal initialisation was found to suppress the L/N fluctuations induced by Gaussian initialisation; a direct treatment of the joint limit, however, remains beyond our present scope. Finally, while our theoretical framework applies to a large class of commonly used activation functions, our numerical validation has, in most cases, been restricted to sine activations. Our results suggest a specific, testable prediction beyond sine activations: a SIREN-like network with sinusoidal input encoding but tanh in the bulk layers, initialised at the corresponding 

tanh critical point, should match the performance of the purely sinusoidal network initialised at criticality. This is consistent with the view that the bulk activation is secondary once a suitable frequency-rich input encoding and critical initialisation are chosen. We leave a systematic test of this prediction to future work. Extending our theoretical treatment to the class of unbounded, scale-invariant activation functions [8], which includes ReLU, and hence the widely used Kaiming initialisation, as a limiting case, would also be worth pursuing.

> 3. Finite Number of Fixed Points for F

Now that we have characterized the diagonal recurrence relation generated by FK , to fully determine the global recurrence, we must also study FC in (B1), the restriction of F

to the off-diagonal elements. 

a. Asymptotic Autonomy First, note that since FC is continuous (as it is a Gaussian integral), we can focus on the limiting autonomous map FC ( · , K ⋆, K ⋆) to analyze the convergence behaviour of the non-autonomous sequence. 

b. Finiteness of the Fixed-Point Set Another key observation is that K⋆ is already a fixed point of FC ( · , K ⋆, K ⋆). Thus, we only need to show that there is a finite number of such fixed points. This follows directly from the analyticity of FC ( · , K ⋆, K ⋆), assuming it is not the identity map. Indeed, the function Kαβ 7 → FC (Kαβ , K ⋆, K ⋆) − Kαβ is analytic on the interval [ −K⋆, K ⋆] and non-zero, so it admits a finite number of fixed points. Therefore, 

FC also has a finite number of fixed points, and the same conclusion holds for the global map F .39 

4. Basin of convergence of the off-diagonal recursion 

Now that we know that there is a finite number of fixed points, let us determine whether their basins of convergence cover the entire admissible covariance interval [ −K⋆, K ⋆]. In other words, we want to show that, for every Kαβ  

> 0

in this interval, the sequence generated by the limiting map converges. Hypothesis ( H4 ) ensures that FC ( · , K ⋆, K ⋆) is increasing. Consequently, the sequence ( Kαβ ℓ )ℓ∈N is monotone for every initial condition. Since it is also bounded, it converges. Therefore, the basins of convergence of these fixed points cover the entire admissible interval. As stated previously for the non-autonomous recursion, the convergence of the diagonal sequences to K⋆, together with the continuity of FC , ensures that the layer-dependent maps asymptotically approach the limiting map, giving the same fixed point and ensuring that the basins of convergence covers the admissible interval. Nevertheless, the two recursions do not necessarily have the same basins of convergence, since the transient dynamics may affect which fixed point is ultimately selected.
