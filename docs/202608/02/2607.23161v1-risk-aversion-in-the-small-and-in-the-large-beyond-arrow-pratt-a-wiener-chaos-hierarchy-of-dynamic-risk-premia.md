## 摘要
Arrow-Pratt近似是期望效用理论的基石之一，它基于绝对风险厌恶提供了确定性等价物和风险溢价的经典局部近似。尽管其应用广泛，但其数学范围以及与高阶风险偏好的关系仍仅被部分理解。本文基于Malliavin微积分和Wiener混沌分析，建立了一个分析确定性等价物和动态风险溢价的新框架。我们首先证明，对于任意趋于零风险的序列，经典的Arrow-Pratt近似在渐近意义上并不总是有效的，从而识别出传统理论的精确局限性。受此观察启发，我们通过布朗滤波考虑不确定性的逐步揭示，动态地表述确定性等价物。结合Itô微积分、Clark--Ocone表示和Wiener混沌分解，我们导出了高阶动态风险溢价的完整层级，并获得了相应系数关于Malliavin导数的显式表示。对于混合Wiener混沌展开，高阶偏好度量（包括谨慎（prudence）和节制（temperance））通过混沌分量之间的相互作用自然出现，并使用Bell多项式表示进行刻画。关于二次高斯泛函和Vasicek利率模型的显式结果说明了该理论，并识别出一大类正则Wiener泛函，对于这些泛函，经典的Arrow-Pratt近似作为首阶项被恢复。这些结果建立了一个统一框架，将期望效用理论、随机分析和Wiener混沌展开联系起来，为高阶确定性等价物和风险的动态测量开辟了新视角。

## Abstract
The Arrow-Pratt approximation is one of the cornerstones of expected utility theory, providing the classical local approximation of certainty equivalents and risk premia in terms of absolute risk aversion. Despite its widespread use, its mathematical scope and relationship to higher-order risk preferences remain only partially understood. This paper develops a new framework for the analysis of certainty equivalents and dynamic risk premia based on Malliavin calculus and Wiener chaos analysis. We first show that the classical Arrow-Pratt approximation is not asymptotically valid for arbitrary sequences of vanishing risks, thereby identifying precise limitations of the traditional theory. Motivated by this observation, we formulate certainty equivalents dynamically by considering the progressive revelation of uncertainty through a Brownian filtration. Combining Itô calculus, the Clark--Ocone representation and the Wiener chaos decomposition, we derive a complete hierarchy of higher-order dynamic risk premia and obtain explicit representations of the corresponding coefficients in terms of Malliavin derivatives. For mixed Wiener chaos expansions, higher-order preference measures, including prudence and temperance, emerge naturally through interactions between chaos components and are characterised using Bell polynomial representations. Explicit results for quadratic Gaussian functionals and the Vasicek interest-rate model illustrate the theory and identify a broad class of regular Wiener functionals for which the classical Arrow-Pratt approximation is recovered as the leading-order term. The results establish a unified framework linking expected utility theory, stochastic analysis and Wiener chaos expansions, opening a new perspective on higher-order certainty equivalents and the dynamic measurement of risk.
