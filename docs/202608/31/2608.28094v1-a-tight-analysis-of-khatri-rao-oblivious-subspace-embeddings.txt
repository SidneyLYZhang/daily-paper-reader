# A Tight Analysis of Khatri-Rao Oblivious Subspace Embeddings 

## Lorenzo Beretta 1 and Cameron Musco 21IBM, lorenzo2beretta@gmail.com

Abstract 

We study random sketching matrices with Khatri-Rao structure. In particular, we consider the Khatri-Rao product (i.e., column-wise tensor product) A1 ⊙ · · · ⊙ Ad ∈ R(n1··· nd)×m of random matri-ces Ai ∈ Rni×m whose columns are isotropic, independent and sub-Gaussian (e.g., Gaussian matrices). Khatri-Rao sketching matrices are widely applied in randomized algorithms for linear algebraic compu-tation and data analysis, when the input data has tensor structure that allows for fast multiplication with A1 ⊙ · · · ⊙ Ad. However, existing theory is not able to fully explain their performance in practice. In particular, despite significant attention, our best bounds for the important oblivious subspace embedding 

property with Khatri-Rao matrices lag behind what is achievable with standard unstructured matrices. For embedding a k-dimensional subspace to (1 ± ε) error, Bujanovi´ c et al. [6] prove that sketching dimension m = O(k3/2/ε 2) suffices in the special case of d = 2. Their dependence on k is weaker than the tight bound of O(k/ε 2) known for unstructured sub-Gaussian sketching matrices. In this work, we close this gap, showing that m = ˜O(k/ε 2) suffices for subspace embedding with a Khatri-Rao sketching matrix with any fixed order d. Our proof is simple, leveraging just two basic properties of the Khatri-Rao sketching distribution: 1) the columns of A1 ⊙ · · · ⊙ Ad ∈ R(n1··· nd)×m are independent and isotropic, and 2) each column of A1 ⊙ · · · ⊙ Ad ∈ R(n1··· nd)×m satisfies a weak Johnson-Lindenstrauss type moment property. 

# 1 Introduction 

Random sketching is a key tool in work on fast algorithms for linear algebraic computation, large-scale data analysis, and low-memory streaming computation [16, 15, 24]. Given an input matrix X ∈ Rn×p, we generate a random sketching matrix Ψ ∈ Rn×m, and compute the sketch Ψ T X ∈ Rm×p. Ψ T X serves as a lossy compression of X, that can be used to approximately solve a breadth of problems, such as low-rank approximation [11], linear regression [19], clustering [10], eigenvalue problems [21], and beyond. In many applications, X is known to be structured – e.g., it may be sparse or may be given as the tensor product of several small matrices. In such applications, it is desirable to use a sketching matrix Ψ that also has structure, that facilitates fast computation of Ψ T X. In applications where X has tensor structure, a key tool in the literature is the Khatri-Rao random sketch [17, 2, 1, 20, 9, 3, 6, 18, 8, 12]. 

Definition 1.1 (Khatri-Rao product) . Let A ∈ Rna×m, B ∈ Rnb×m have columns A = [ a1, . . . , a m]and B = [ b1, . . . , b m]. Their Khatri-Rao product is the column-wise Kronecker product 

A ⊙ B := a1 ⊗ b1, · · · , am ⊗ bm

In this work we consider a sketching matrix Ψ = 1√m A1 ⊙ · · · ⊙ Ad that is the Khatri-Rao product of sketching matrices Ai ∈ Rni×m, appropriately scaled. We will focus on the common case when all Ai

have independent sub-Gaussian columns, which of course includes independent standard Gaussian matrices. O(( n1+· · · +nd)·dm ) time, as compared to O(n1 · · · nd)·dm ) time for a dense unstructured random projection. This represents an exponential savings in cost. For this reason, Khatri-Rao sketches have found applications in numerous settings, ranging from contour integral-based eigensolvers [6], to compression algorithms for massive tensors and tensor networks, arising e.g., in quantum physics [7, 12], to approximation algorithms for polynomial kernels [2, 1], and more [14, 8, 9, 5]
